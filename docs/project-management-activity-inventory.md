# Project-management activity inventory

Date: 2026-11-03. Current status: the discipline inventory informed the subsequent AI-led tailoring and owner-authorized toolkit source implementation. [Evaluation](../evals/project-manage-assessment.md) records its bounded evidence; actual application adoption and optimization remain untested. The original discipline review below preceded those choices and is retained separately from the later tailoring/decision sections. The inventory itself is not a checklist to implement wholesale.

## Scope and source boundaries

PMP is a professional certification, not a single prescribed delivery workflow. PMI's [Process Groups: A Practice Guide](https://pmi.bookstore.ipgbook.com/process-groups--a-practice-guide-products-9781628257830.php?page_id=30) describes a predictive baseline of 49 processes across five groups. The [PMBOK Eighth Edition contents](https://www.pmi.org/-/media/pmi/documents/public/pdf/publications/pmbok-guide-eighth-edition_table-of-contents.pdf) organizes the discipline through seven performance domains and addresses predictive, adaptive and hybrid delivery. These are overlapping views, not interchangeable lists or consecutive project phases.

The inventory below covers all 49 baseline process responsibilities in independently phrased operational language, then adds explicit leadership, business and adaptive responsibilities. It is a practical synthesis, not a reproduction of PMI's process descriptions, input/tool/output tables, or a claim to enumerate every possible action in every industry. The public book description verifies the model/count; individual practical explanations are this inventory's interpretation. Formal process names and complete ITTO detail should be checked in the licensed practice guide when an exact formal mapping is required. Current PMP coverage is separately checked in section 4 rather than assuming that the 49-process view exhausts the profession.

An activity is work or a decision. A register, plan or dashboard is a possible record of that work. One activity may use several records; one record may support several activities. These lists do not require 49 documents or sequential execution.

## 1. Lifecycle responsibilities

| Group | Responsibility | Typical decision or evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initiating | Establish the purpose, authorized boundary, sponsorship and affected parties. | Whether to undertake the project/phase and who is accountable. |
| Planning | Choose a coherent approach for achieving outcomes within constraints and uncertainty. | What work to do, how, with whom, in what order and within what tolerances. |
| Executing | Coordinate delivery, people, resources, suppliers, information and agreed responses. | Actual deliverables, resolved impediments and operational decisions. |
| Monitoring and controlling | Compare evidence with intent, assess implications and take authorized action. | Verified status, revised forecasts, acceptance and controlled changes. |
| Closing | Conclude the boundary responsibly and transfer its outcomes and obligations. | Acceptance/termination, handover, reconciled commitments and retained learning. |

These responsibilities overlap and recur. Planning continues as evidence changes; monitoring accompanies delivery; a phase or increment can close before the entire project ends.

## 3. Responsibilities that need explicit attention beyond a process-name checklist

The following is this inventory's practical decomposition. Many activities overlap the baseline above; they are not  additional official numbered PMI processes. They prevent the baseline from being reduced to document administration.

### Purpose, value and business stewardship

- Understand the problem/opportunity, alternatives and rationale for investment.
- Relate project outcomes to organizational objectives and identify intended beneficiaries.
- Establish what benefits mean, how they can be observed and who owns realization after handover.
- Test whether priorities and delivery options still serve that rationale as conditions change.
- Consider whether to continue, redirect, pause or terminate when the business justification no longer holds.

### Governance, ethics and accountability

- Clarify sponsorship, decision rights, delegated authority, escalation and exception handling.
- Establish appropriate review boundaries, phase decisions and auditability of consequential choices.
- Protect confidential information and handle access, conflicts of interest and reported concerns responsibly.
- Identify applicable obligations and obtain competent interpretation where necessary; evidence compliance instead of assuming it.
- Consider relevant environmental, social and economic consequences of project choices.
- Maintain human accountability for decisions supported by automation or AI; check data suitability, confidentiality and output reliability.

### Leadership and human collaboration

- Establish shared purpose, working expectations and clear responsibilities.
- Build trust, inclusion and an environment where people can report uncertainty or failure.
- Coach, mentor, negotiate and resolve disagreements; adapt leadership to context.
- Support learning, motivation and sustainable workload; address capability and capacity limits.
- Work with sponsor/customer/operations partners rather than assume the project manager can make every decision.

### Adaptive and hybrid delivery

- Select and revisit the delivery approach based on uncertainty, product characteristics and organizational needs.
- Maintain and refine a prioritized backlog where appropriate; clarify readiness and completion expectations.
- Plan releases/iterations or continuous flow, balancing feedback, dependencies and capacity.
- Demonstrate usable increments and incorporate stakeholder feedback into subsequent choices.
- Track and address excessive work in progress, bottlenecks, handoff delays and aging blocked work where relevant.
- Coordinate cross-team dependencies without assuming that team-level progress guarantees integrated delivery.
- Combine adaptive work with fixed contractual, regulatory, funding or operational obligations coherently.

### Issues, assumptions and integration

- Distinguish an uncertain risk, an existing issue, an assumption and an accepted decision.
- Assign resolution ownership, assess consequences and follow through on blockers.
- Test assumptions early enough to affect investment and sequencing decisions.
- Reconcile contradictions across plans, records, stakeholders and actual delivery.
- Check system-level interfaces and readiness across deliverables, suppliers and operations.

### Measurement, forecasting and improvement

- Choose measures that support actual decisions; define their meaning, source and limitations.
- Keep commitments, observed actuals and revised forecasts distinguishable.
- Evaluate scope, quality, schedule, resource and financial consequences together.
- Separate observed outcome improvements from reporting improvements and unsupported attribution.
- Diagnose causes of variance, select corrective actions, observe their results and retain useful learning.
- Reassess reporting and control effort so management practices remain useful and proportionate.

### Transition, adoption and benefits follow-through

- Check operational readiness, service ownership, training, support and unresolved obligations.
- Understand adoption impacts and coordinate organizational change with the responsible parties.
- Transfer knowledge, assets and continuing risks/issues with accountable recipients.
- Confirm acceptance or document the reason for cancellation/termination.
- Reconcile commitments and preserve records, then transfer benefits measurement to its continuing owner.

## 6. Tailoring for AI-led delivery with a human client/product owner

Owner direction: select project-management activities for better outcomes with minimum governance. The agent performs most delivery responsibilities; the owner supplies requirements and retains client/product decisions to the appropriate extent. This section proposes a tailored operating model, not implementation or a mandatory process for every task. The AI/tool-only financial scope remains unchanged.

PMI's [tailoring guidance](https://www.pmi.org/-/media/pmi/documents/public/pdf/pmbok-standards/pmi-tailoring.pdf) supports adjusting practices for organizational needs, project criticality and context, then inspecting and improving them. The selections below are this project's proposed application of that principle; PMI has not prescribed this particular AI-led model. [PMI's AI guidance](https://www.pmi.org/blog/ai-for-project-managers-beyond-quick-wins) also emphasizes continuing human accountability.

### Operating assumptions and role boundaries

Treat the agent as a combined delivery function: analysis, planning, design, construction, verification, coordination and reporting can share execution responsibility. Do not create a simulated management hierarchy or separate approver for each role. Expertise and evidence boundaries still matter; selecting a different skill is not an organizational handoff or proof of independence.

The owner retains purpose, value priorities, consequential scope/trade-offs, spending/authority boundaries and product acceptance where reserved. The agent makes technical and sequencing decisions within established delegation, verifies explicit acceptance conditions and escalates a decision only when outside that authority or requiring unavailable owner judgment. Existing delegation persists; no per-task reauthorization is implied.

Combining delivery roles does not make the agent every stakeholder. Real end users, service owners, suppliers, regulators or operators may have interests and authority even if they are absent. The agent can analyze their likely needs, but must distinguish assumptions from their actual evidence, consent or acceptance. Likewise, one agent's self-review is not independent assurance. Fresh reviewers can challenge assumptions when warranted, but shared models/tools/instructions still limit independence.

### Selection rule

For each candidate control, identify the outcome it protects, failure it prevents, lightest effective action, trigger and authority, then compare expected decision/risk benefit with execution, upkeep and owner-interruption cost. These are hypotheses to observe, not numerical scores invented from this discussion. Keep, combine, adapt or activate conditionally; omit a ceremony when its real-world purpose is absent. Do not remove a safety/authority requirement merely because there is one operator.

### Candidate core for substantial projects

| Control | Outcome protected | Lightest useful behavior | Trigger / owner involvement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shared outcome, acceptance and delegation | Work solves the intended problem with a legitimate completion decision. | Recover the agreed boundary and success evidence; resolve material ambiguity without a standard questionnaire. Reuse the existing contract. | At inception/recovery or a material intent change; owner supplies value/authority decisions. |
| Coherent next path and forecast | The next useful outcome has a feasible dependency/capacity path and visible schedule/spend implications. | Maintain near-term deliverables, prerequisites, critical uncertainties and a conditional forecast. Keep commitments, actuals and estimates distinct. Track AI/tool cost with known coverage. | Before substantial commitments and after material progress/dependency changes; routine planning belongs to the agent. |
| Risk-led sequencing and bounded investment | Significant uncertainty is resolved before disproportionate dependent work or irreversible effects. | Address the decision-changing assumption with suitable evidence; pursue independent useful work while blocked; choose a work size that can be completed and checked. | When uncertainty or impact warrants it; owner only for a trade-off outside existing delegation. |
| Evidence-backed completion and proportionate challenge | Implementation or self-consistency is not mistaken for the correct outcome. | Verify against agreed, independently grounded expectations. Use appropriate real-boundary evidence and targeted fresh challenge for consequential correlated-error risk. Preserve blocked/unavailable proof. | At completion of affected behavior and material changes; owner product judgment at useful milestones where required. |
| Exception and change control | Scope, cost, date and quality trade-offs remain deliberate without blocking routine work. | Compare proposed change with current intent/constraints, act within authority, retain consequential decisions and update affected forecasts. Communicate exceptions with a recommendation. | Material deviation/change, exhausted authority or unavailable owner input; no change board or routine approval loop. |
| Learning applied to the next decision | Repeated failure and waste lead to a useful corrective action. | Compare expected and observed outcomes, identify the actual constraint, apply a bounded intervention and assess its result with quality guardrails. | A repeated problem, consequential surprise or meaningful milestone; no mandatory separate retrospective ceremony. |

These controls describe outcomes and decisions, not six required files, six skills or six steps for every increment. A current project view is a low-cost expression of the same facts, not another parallel reporting workflow. For a trivial correction, most of the above is already settled and need not be reopened.

### Disposition of the complete baseline

| Baseline IDs | Disposition in this operating model | Reason / condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01–07 integration | Retain and combine. | One delivery function reconciles planning, execution, knowledge, change and closure; preserve real owner authority rather than inventing internal authorization ceremonies. |
| 08–13 scope | Retain proportionately. | Correct intent and acceptance remain essential. Use existing requirements/backlog; decompose enough for useful increments. No mandatory scope document set. |
| 14–19 schedule | Adapt to rolling forecasts. | Fewer human handoffs do not remove external waiting, tool limits, dependencies or rework. Detail the next path; avoid pseudo-precise whole-project dates. |
| 20–23 cost | Adapt to AI/tool-only finance. | Respect the selected cost boundary, use actual charges or explicit estimates and retain attribution/coverage gaps. No invented human labor budget. |
| 24–26 quality | Retain; strengthen evidence boundaries when roles are combined. | The same assumption may contaminate design, implementation and checks. Ground expectations separately and target review at consequence, rather than doubling every task. |
| 27–29 and 32 resource activities | Adapt to actual capacity/access. | Manage tool availability, contexts, expertise, safe concurrency, environments and real external resources. Do not assume unlimited capacity or create an agent-role roster for its own sake. |
| 30–31 human team development/management | Omit human-team ceremonies when no human delivery team exists. | Motivation, appraisal and interpersonal coaching do not become agent-management rituals. Keep actual delivery coordination; revisit if people join. |
| 33–35 communications | Compress into decision-useful visibility and exceptions. | Owner can recover position/path without rereading chats; targeted updates replace standups, handoff meetings and repeated status narration. |
| 36–38 and 40–42 risk | Combine into risk-informed work decisions and follow-through. | Surface consequential threats/opportunities, prioritize, choose/execute responses and revisit them; no separate risk paperwork by default. |
| 39 quantitative risk analysis | Conditional. | Use only when credible data and decision consequence justify the analysis; simulation without grounded assumptions supplies no reliability. |
| 43–45 procurement | Conditional on actual acquisition/supplier commitments. | Paid tools, licenses or external providers may require this work. Do not simulate negotiations or imply authority to purchase. |
| 46–49 stakeholders | Simplify around actual affected parties. | One owner may combine client/product roles, but absent users or external authorities are not imaginary agent roles. Reassess if their decisions are necessary. |

Business value, ethics, compliance, organizational change, adoption and transition remain outcome responsibilities. Activate relevant work when the project creates those consequences; do not exclude it solely because delivery is automated. Check acceptance and handover at the right boundary without manufacturing additional stakeholder approvals.

### Minimum governance versus under-management

Routine technical choices, decomposition and resequencing remain delegated. Owner questions should concern purpose/value conflicts, material scope or tolerance changes, actual spending/access authority, irreversible consequences outside delegation or reserved product acceptance. A reported estimate change alone is not a new permission requirement. Preserve existing project and tool permissions.

Raise control intensity for higher impact, irreversibility, uncertainty, external obligations, conflicting evidence or repeated failures. Reduce it for understood, reversible, contained work. First combine/reuse existing evidence and records; introduce a new artifact, reviewer or tool only for a specific unresolved control need. No universal complexity score, numeric escalation threshold or extra approval gate is adopted here.

Measure usefulness by time to acceptable outcomes, avoidable blocked time/rework, owner correction/decision burden, AI/tool spend with known coverage and quality failures. Include management overhead in the evaluation. Fewer documents, tokens, questions or elapsed minutes alone do not establish better delivery. There is no evidence yet that this candidate selection maximizes benefit; that is the objective to test, not an achieved result.
