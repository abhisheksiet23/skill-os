## Devil's-advocate follow-up

Owner requested a challenge of the implementation, then clarified that the discussion is exploratory and nothing is binding. This review therefore prioritizes practical usefulness over hypothetical budget-enforcement mechanisms. No skill changes, new experiments, external access or application edits were performed. This is the author's same-context critique, not another independent review.

- **Demonstrated gain is narrower than the capability description.** The candidate improved incomplete-cost wording and separation of project status from session scores. Baseline already recovered most state, schedule, authority and restraint cases competently. These results do not demonstrate better task selection, forecasting, delivery or optimization from adding management expertise.
- **Tests prompt the behavior that needs to become reliable during work.** A1 explicitly requests a status update, and A2 explicitly supplies a change event. Neither establishes unprompted upkeep during ordinary implementation, later fresh-session recovery or whether the owner remains oriented without asking. Adoption guidance partially addresses activation in source but has not been operationally exercised.
- **Caution can outgrow decision support.** Honest unknowns are necessary, but the cases reward refusing unsupported full-project dates more than producing useful estimates. The missing test is an adequately evidenced controllable increment where provisional forecasting, estimate revision and a sequencing decision should actually occur. An external uncertainty should not obscure what can be forecast independently.
- **Independent work is not automatically the best next work.** The skill says useful independent work may proceed, which preserves flexibility. Execution still needs to compare its outcome value with resolving the milestone-driving blocker, clarifying a requirement or stopping low-value work. No matched task demonstrated a superior allocation decision.
- **Minimum governance remains an unmeasured claim.** A 21-skill library now has management reasoning, context upkeep and mandatory adopted-session assessments. Their boundaries are coherent, but repeated source reads, record reconciliation and owner interpretation costs have not been compared with simpler practice. Better caveats and more complete records alone do not justify that overhead.
- **Cost evidence and oversight are still separate capabilities.** No actual project-attributable spend source was inspected. The method can reconcile supplied data but has not delivered real spend visibility. If a future project introduces a genuinely binding limit with incomplete actuals, the current general delegation/cost wording should be checked for a safe agreed fallback; this is a conditional edge case, not an existing owner budget violation or a reason to add a new gate now.

What survives: combined delivery roles, real human authority, reuse of records, protection against fabricated certainty and proportionate review are sound design choices. The critique does not justify rolling them back or expanding the framework.

Revised confidence: **reasonable small management candidate; project-outcome benefit unproved**. The most useful next observation is a normal project increment, not another broad design pass: can the agent choose a high-value next action, keep position/path current without an explicit status request, offer useful conditional estimates where possible, and reduce owner reconstruction/corrections without disproportionate upkeep? Observe any actual optimization decision and its outcome separately. Simplify or retire optional guidance if it adds no value; preserve necessary evidence and authority protections. No new acceptance commitment is inferred from this exploratory review.
