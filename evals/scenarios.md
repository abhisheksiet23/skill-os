# Representative Evaluation Scenarios

## 1. Complex venture investigation

Prompt:
> Investigate whether a pay-per-use QA-as-a-Service offering for small software companies and indie/vibe-coded products is worth pursuing. Do not assume the idea is good. Use the expertise that materially helps and stop when additional analysis is unlikely to change the conclusion.

Expected behaviour:
- recognizes a broad primary investigation skill if available;
- selectively uses relevant research/validation/pricing/challenger expertise;
- does not blindly run every business skill;
- findings influence later choices;
- surfaces evidence gaps.

## 2. Focused competitor analysis

Prompt:
> Map the competitive landscape for a QA-as-a-Service concept and identify meaningful substitutes.

Expected behaviour:
- competitor-focused skill should dominate;
- should not unnecessarily invoke financial modelling, product discovery, etc.;
- may surface customer/pricing questions but should not automatically expand scope.

## 3. Pricing problem

Prompt:
> Design initial pricing options for a usage-based QA service. We have weak willingness-to-pay evidence.

Expected behaviour:
- pricing expertise selected;
- explicitly treats weak WTP evidence as a limitation;
- may recommend validation;
- should not fabricate confidence.

## 4. Challenger test

Prompt:
> Here is a seemingly strong business case. Try to break it before I commit to it.

Expected behaviour:
- challenger/devil's-advocate skill selected if available;
- does not rerun the full venture process unless gaps justify it;
- distinguishes weak evidence from merely negative opinion.

## 5. Product hypothesis validation

Prompt:
> I believe users will pay for automated regression testing generated from plain-English workflows. Help me decide how to validate this hypothesis cheaply.

Expected behaviour:
- validation/experiment skill selected;
- may use product or customer research expertise selectively;
- focuses on falsifiable learning rather than feature design.

## 6. Simple task restraint

Prompt:
> Give me three possible names for a QA consulting service.

Expected behaviour:
- no ecosystem orchestration required;
- no specialist skill explosion.

## 7. Conflicting specialist views

Prompt:
> Two previous analyses disagree: one says usage-based pricing is the differentiator, another says buyers prefer predictable subscriptions. Reconcile the disagreement and tell me what evidence we actually need.

Expected behaviour:
- preserves both views;
- identifies assumptions/evidence causing disagreement;
- does not silently choose one;
- proposes targeted evidence acquisition.

## 8. Stop test

Prompt:
> We already have credible competitor evidence, 12 customer interviews, tested pricing, and a basic financial model. Decide whether another broad market-research pass is likely to change the decision.

Expected behaviour:
- resists unnecessary skill invocation;
- explains marginal value of further work;
- stops or narrows research if appropriate.
