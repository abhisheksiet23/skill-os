# Market Research

Answer a business decision with a defensible view of the alternatives buyers can actually choose.

## Terms
- **Alternative:** anything a buyer can actually choose, including doing nothing.
- **Primary source:** the origin of a claim, not a repetition of it.
- **Strategy Canvas:** a comparison of the factors customers experience across meaningful alternatives.

## Always
1. Keep unknowns unknown. Missing information is not zero.
2. A gap in sampled offers does not show unmet demand, and differentiation does not prove demand.
3. Never send private customer material to public research tools.
4. Never fabricate findings.
5. Stay within the request. Add no pricing design, financial model or full venture investigation automatically.

## Frame the question
Before researching:
- Define: the decision, segment, geography and relevant time frame.
- Supplied evidence: inspect it first. Identify which claims need current verification.
- Focus: keep a focused competitor request focused, even when other business questions exist.

## Build a relevant comparison set
- Reuse: use supplied current sources where their scope fits.
- Search: work in focused batches around the unresolved buyer choice.
- Stop adding alternatives when they no longer change that choice.
- Cover what customers actually use: direct competitors, adjacent offers, in-house work, freelancers, free tools and doing nothing.
- Compare: use the job and buying situation, not feature counts alone.
- Buyers: separate user from payer.
- Also identify: switching costs and channels.

## Establish what each source can support
- Sources: use current primary sources where possible.
- Consequential claims: triangulate them.
- Record: source and date, offer scope, qualifications and uncertainty.
- Repeats: several sites repeating one estimate are one underlying source, not independent support.
- Vendor page: it establishes the advertised offer. It does not establish effectiveness, market share, revenue or profitability.
- Unlike prices: units, service boundaries, regions or commitments may differ. Show the normalization, or leave the offers explicitly incomparable.
- Contradiction: seek it, and sample beyond the most visible companies.

Do not read one thing as another:

| Evidence | Does not show |
| --- | --- |
| Search visibility | Market dominance |
| Interview statements | Behavior |
| Analyst estimates | Observed transactions |

## Use the reference
Read [buyer-choice contrasts, value curves and sizing](references/value-curves.md) when either applies:
- Explanations of why buyers switch conflict.
- A Strategy Canvas or market bound would change the decision.

## Report and stop

Write one business document, not a separate report for each question.

### One document
- Find first: use the project's business document or equivalent records. Otherwise create `business.md` in the project's records.
- Short question: answer it and its limit in chat. Create no document for it.
- No write access, or a read-only request: give the changed sections in chat.
- Read the whole document before changing it. Keep its IDs, history and wording that still holds.
- Add only sections that hold something useful. Number and order them as below.
- One home per item. Elsewhere, cite its section or ID.

| # | Section | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Decision and next steps | The one current summary |
| 2 | Current test | The one test: pass marks, limits, stops and results |
| 3 | Customers and offer fit | Needs linked to the offer |
| 4 | Alternatives and positioning | Choices and proof of difference |
| 5 | Reaching customers | Who, where, message, follow-up and effort |
| 6 | Offer and price | Packages, terms and bills |
| 7 | Business model | Canvas, numbers, capacity and cash |
| 8 | Assumptions and risks | One ordered list and the countercase |
| 9 | Records | Facts, owner decisions, open items, evidence, conflicts, replaced items |

### Your sections and others
- Yours: sections 3 and 4.
- Write yours in full, using the templates below.
- Another section: add only supported information it lacks. Otherwise do not reword or shorten it.
- Test changes go in section 2. New risks go in section 8.
- Section 1: update it when your evidence changes the answer, actions or warnings. Keep one summary.

### Changing what is recorded
- Different judgment: add a conflict to section 9. Do not rewrite the other conclusion. Show it in section 1 while unresolved.
- Checked error: you recomputed it from recorded inputs, or confirmed it against its cited source.
- A different assumption or method is a judgment, not an error.
- Checked calculation or factual error: correct it where it appears, marked "Corrected" with the date. Update what depends on it.
- For each correction, record the old value, new value and basis in section 9.
- Error not yet checked: keep the value, flag it beside the value and record a conflict.
- Correction never covers owner statements, owner decisions, approved terms, pass marks or recorded results. Flag those and ask the owner.
- Pass marks: change them in section 2 only, before results. After results, start a new test and keep the old one.
- Replaced item: move it to the replaced list with the reason. Do not silently delete it.

### Records
- IDs: F fact, D owner decision, O open item, E evidence, X conflict, numbered, such as F-1. Keep an existing document's own scheme.
- Give each record its source and date.
- A recommendation never becomes an owner decision.

### Section 1 template
| Line | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Recommendation** | The current answer and its main reason |
| Why | Up to three reasons |
| Against | The strongest case against, even when recommending a stop |
| Would change if | The observation that would change the answer |
| At risk | Time, money, obligations, and the loss limit with what counts toward it |
| Cost if wrong | What is lost if the answer is wrong, and who bears it |
| Stop | What ends or pauses the current step |
| Unknown | Only what could change the answer |

Below the table:
- **Next, in order:** actions for the immediate step, up to the owner's next decision.
- **You decide:** approvals needed now, each with a default. A default grants no permission to spend, contact or launch.
- **Warnings:** one line each for this step.

The owner can complete the step from section 1 and the test it names.

### Shared layouts
Use these whoever writes the section. Add no second layout.

**Section 2, test card:**

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| We believe | Falsifiable hypothesis |
| Who | Eligible audience, recruitment, exposure |
| We will | Method and offer |
| We measure | Observable action and counts kept separate |
| Right if | Success signal and threshold |
| Wrong if | Failure signal and threshold |
| Inconclusive if | When access or measurement cannot tell |
| Bound | Time and cost |
| Then | Action for each result |

Anything unmet or unchecked that must be true before the owner offers, sells or delivers, including to prevent harm, is a step row at the earliest step it blocks. It is never only a note, risk or open item. Stages and added rules use the same table:

| Step | Limit | Pass | Fail | Inconclusive | Then |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

- Put stages, price tests, guardrails, reversal and kill criteria in this table or the card.
- Reversal or kill row: observation under Step, date under Limit, effect under Then.
- Add no other table to section 2. A learning card for results is the only exception.

**Section 3, customer needs:**

| Need, as a job, pain or gain | Priority and why | Current way | Offer part that serves it | Evidence tag | Check by asking about past behavior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

**Section 8, one list:**

| Assumption or claim | Evidence tag, source and limit | If it fails | Resolving check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Say whether a risk is manageable.

### Evidence tags
| Tag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Known | Directly observed, or stated by the owner about their own business. Say which |
| Supported | Evidence points to it, within stated limits |
| Assumed | Not yet checked; used to proceed |
| Unknown | No basis yet |
| Contradicted | Evidence or arithmetic goes against it |

An owner's statement does not show how customers behave. Never present a guess as fact.

Calculated figures: mark them "calculated" and give them the evidence tag of their weakest input.

### Length and style
- Limits: section 1, 250 words. Sections 2 to 8, 400 words each. Tables count.
- May exceed a limit: warnings, stop conditions, the canvas and required records.
- Before saving: count each section you changed and cut it to its limit.
- Order: main choice first, then only what changes the decision.
- Calculations: show the deciding inputs and results in a table, each formula once.
- Detailed calculations and extra examples: add them only when the owner asks.
- Writing: fragments, lists and tables in plain words. A sentence only for cause, trade-off or warning.
- Leave out introductions, recaps and method notes.
- Table cell: one idea, 15 words at most.
- A later-stage warning sits beside its stage.

### Chat reply
After writing the document, reply in five short lines at most, one sentence each:
- Changed: what is new or different, and where.
- Answer: the current recommendation.
- Decide: the one decision the owner must make now.
- Watch: the most serious warning, only if one applies.
- Not verified: the biggest gap.

No tables, figures lists or reasons. The document holds them. Add the file path.

### Your content
**Section 3.** First line: **Implication**, what the findings mean for the decision. Then buyer, job and trigger, and the shared needs layout.

**Section 4, alternatives:**

| Alternative, including doing nothing | Price and unit | Why chosen | Why not | Evidence tag, source and date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

**Section 4, positioning:**

| Competitive alternative | What you do differently | Value to the buyer | Who cares most | Proof |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

A difference without proof stays a hypothesis.

**Only when it changes the decision or the owner asks:**
- Switching forces, each with evidence and tag: push from the current way, pull of the new offer, worry about switching, habit.
- Strategy Canvas: label factors and scores observed, sourced or hypothesized. Keep proposed curves separate. Show eliminate, reduce, raise and create.
- Market bound: total, addressable and reachable.

**Section 8:** add decisive unknowns to the shared list. The resolving check names the next research step and what it changes.

### Always
Stop when the decision-relevant alternatives and uncertainties are covered.

When sources are unavailable, report the gap and a bounded research plan.

When demand validation, pricing or a broad venture investigation could resolve a specific question:
- State the question. The agent decides whether to bring in that expertise.
- Invoke no other skill. Do not expand scope automatically.

Structure adapted in our own words from Competing Against Luck (Christensen and colleagues), the switching forces popularized by Bob Moesta, Obviously Awesome (Dunford) and Blue Ocean Strategy (Kim and Mauborgne).

**Wording:** be truthful and growth-minded. Say "not yet" where something can still happen, and keep evidence, warnings and results exact.
