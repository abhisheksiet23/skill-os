# Market Research

Answer a business decision with a defensible view of the alternatives buyers can actually choose. Define the decision, segment, geography and relevant time frame. Inspect supplied evidence before researching and identify which claims need current verification. A focused competitor request should stay focused even when other business questions exist.

## Build a relevant comparison set

Reuse supplied current sources where their scope fits. Search in focused batches around the unresolved buyer choice and stop adding alternatives when they no longer change it. Search for what customers actually use: direct competitors, adjacent offers, in-house work, freelancers, free tools and doing nothing. Compare the job and buying situation rather than feature counts alone. Separate user from payer and identify switching costs and channels.

## Establish what each source can support

Use current primary sources where possible and triangulate consequential claims. Record source/date, offer scope, qualifications and uncertainty. Several sites repeating one estimate are one underlying source, not independent corroboration. A vendor page establishes its advertised offer, not effectiveness, market share, revenue or profitability. Avoid comparing prices with different units, service boundaries, regions or commitments as if equivalent; show the normalization or leave unlike offers explicitly incomparable. Missing information is unknown, not zero.

Seek contradictory evidence and sample beyond the easiest visible companies. Distinguish search visibility from market dominance, interview statements from behavior and analyst estimates from observed transactions. Do not send private customer material to public research tools.

## Translate findings into the decision

Use [buyer-choice contrasts, value curves and sizing](references/value-curves.md) when switching explanations conflict or a canvas/market bound would change the decision. Lead with **Implication**, then compare alternatives using the buyer factors that change the decision, normalized units and nearby source/date links. Unknowns stay unknown; distinguish sampled offer gaps from unmet demand. Keep a short list of decisive uncertainties instead of a source-by-source narrative. For a Strategy Canvas, label observed/sourced/hypothesized factors and scores, separate proposed from current curves, and explain eliminate/reduce/raise/create trade-offs; differentiation is not demand proof. Preserve assumptions in project records.

Stop when decision-relevant alternatives and uncertainties are covered. If sources are unavailable, report the gap and a bounded research plan, not fabricated findings. Optionally recommend `business-validate`, `business-pricing` or `venture-investigate` with a specific question; the agent decides. Do not invoke skills or expand scope automatically.
