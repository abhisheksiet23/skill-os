# Value curves and market bounds

A Strategy Canvas compares factors customers experience across meaningful alternatives. Derive factors from buyer behavior, credible research or clearly marked hypotheses. Keep the current market picture separate from a proposed value curve. Qualitative/ordinal comparisons need a stated basis; unknown values remain unknown, not invented scores.

Use eliminate, reduce, raise and create to ask where the market overprovides, underprovides or misses value. Raising every factor generally raises cost and complexity; show the trade-off and delivery implications. A distinctive curve generates a hypothesis, not proof of uncontested demand. Revise it after observed choice, payment or repeat behavior.

For market sizing, establish rough bounds before elaborate models. Use bottom-up reachable customer counts, plausible purchase frequency and comparable spend; label assumptions and avoid false precision. Distinguish the broad market, the addressable segment under the actual offer, and the share channels/capacity could plausibly reach. Do not assume an arbitrary percentage of a huge market proves an opportunity.

The technique is optional: a concise evidence-backed alternatives table may answer the question better than a canvas or market model.
