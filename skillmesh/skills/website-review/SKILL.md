# Website Review

Find what is stopping the right customers from choosing this business, and show the owner which changes are most likely to help, and why. Return a candidate-bound judgment with actionable evidence, keeping readiness, verification coverage and publication authority separate. A review cannot show that a change will win customers. Only real visitor behavior can.

## Terms
- **Right customer:** someone the business can serve well and wants to win.
- **First look:** a review made without the owner's account of the right customer, where the judgment depends on it.
- **Candidate:** the specific version of the site that is being built, reviewed or released.
- **Candidate-bound:** tied to one specific candidate. If the candidate changes, the evidence may no longer apply.
- **Findability:** how readily search engines and AI assistants locate the site's pages and describe them accurately.
- **Blocking finding:** a false or unsupported central claim, a broken primary journey (including one that keyboard or assistive-technology users cannot complete), exposed secrets or private data, or another defect that defeats safe intended use.
- **Readiness:** needs changes means a Blocking finding is open. Not yet determinable means none is known, but the main conversion path or central claims are unchecked. Ready means both are checked and no Blocking finding is open. Other problems may remain. Readiness does not show that the site wins the right customers or that a redesign is unnecessary.
- **Checked conversion path:** followed as far as the environment and authority safely allow. Later steps, such as receipt, are named verification gaps.
- **Verification:** complete, or incomplete with named gaps. It is independent of readiness: a candidate can have observed defects and untested areas together.
- **Representative journey:** one complete entry-to-action path used early to expose systemic issues.

## Frame the review
Do this first.
- Identify: the URL or revision, environment, requested scope and success criteria.
- Brief: use existing briefs and business records. Do not require a discovery project.
- Right customer: state who they are, what brings them and what else they could choose, including doing nothing. Say whether each comes from evidence or your assumption, because the whole review depends on it.
- Scope: respect review-only scope.
- Independence: review the target and the raw evidence, not the author's desired conclusion. If you authored what you review, say so and mark the review non-independent.

## Get the business understanding first
A site shows what a business says, not whom it needs to win or why they choose it. Use this when the review will judge customer fit, what to change first or whether to replace the site, and no brief, record or owner answer covers whom to win, why they choose the business and what they should do next. A narrow check, such as accessibility or broken links, does not need it.
- Look first: take a quick look, enough to say what the site appears to claim about its customer and why that cannot settle the review. Do not rank changes or judge readiness yet.
- Ask: when someone can answer, send what you saw, any serious fault already found and why the answers matter. Then ask only what is not known, in plain words, and wait:
  1. Whom do you want more of, and who is a poor fit?
  2. What brought recent good customers, and what else did they consider?
  3. Why did they choose you? What did they say or do that shows it?
  4. What should a visitor do next, and what happens after that?
  5. Why look at the site now? What is not working, and what must stay?
- Extra questions: add at most two short ones, only to settle a serious fault or central claims already found. Leave other details until later.
- Go-between: the person asking may not be the owner. Make the questions easy to forward, and keep that person's guesses apart from the owner's answers.
- Push back once: if asked to go on without answers, say briefly what the review cannot tell, then do a first look. Do the same without waiting when nobody can answer or the request already rules out questions. Never withhold the review.
- First look: call it that at the top. Observed faults stand. Customer fit, reasons to choose and the order of changes are provisional. Do not judge whether to keep, redesign or rebuild the site. Say which answers that decision needs.
- First-look claims: a claim you cannot check without the owner is unverified, which leaves readiness not yet determinable. This does not apply to a claim the evidence shows to be false or the site itself contradicts. Any other Blocking finding still means needs changes.
- Owner questions: put those still unanswered in the short summary, each with what its answer could change.
- Beyond the owner: for what the owner cannot answer, recommend the business work below. If the audience or offer proves unsettled, recommend website discovery by name and say why.
- Later answers: revisit the judgments they affect. Keep checks that still apply.

## Follow the customer's decision
A site can work in every part and still give nobody a reason to choose it.
- Find: do searches, referrals and campaigns lead to a page that matches what brought the visitor?
- Fit: does the right customer recognize their situation quickly, and can others tell it is not for them? Honest limits help the right ones choose.
- Reason: why this business over the alternatives? A missing reason can matter more than several visible flaws.
- Belief: is proof beside the claim it supports?
- Hesitation: which concerns about price, risk, effort or what happens next go unanswered?
- Action: is the next step clear, does it work and does it reach the business?
- Effect: judge copy and design by whether they help this customer notice, understand, trust and act, not by taste.
- Confidence: separate customer evidence, what you observed and what you infer. You are not the customer.
- Quality of leads: do not recommend pressure or vagueness to get more enquiries.

## Inspect the actual experience
- Guidance: for a substantial review read the relevant sections of [review guidance](references/review-guide.md), including entry and task selection when choosing a sample.
- Order: inspect a representative journey early. Then extend to other page types, unusual states and all business-critical journeys.
- Reuse: reuse trustworthy proof for unchanged parts of the same candidate. Concentrate new inspection on changed, shared or unverified behavior. Sampling still needs an explicit boundary.
- Together: assess story, fit, proof, copy, imagery, hierarchy, responsive composition, accessibility, functionality, findability and operating ownership. Attractive generic copy is not a credible offer.
- Layouts: render and examine narrow, intermediate and wide layouts at recorded sizes.
- Interaction: exercise navigation, calls to action, keyboard and focus, form validation and recovery.
- Evidence: distinguish build results, screenshots and actual receipt.
- Delivery tests: external ones need authorization. Missing tools or credentials leave their checks unverified.

Source readability, only when implementation or source is in scope:
- Standard: readable for an owner returning after six years.
- Look for: blank lines between logical blocks and meaningful UI sections, cohesive groups, expanded guards, branches, error handling and object literals, visible template nesting, and separated CSS rules.
- An attractive page or a formatter pass does not establish source readability.
- Exclude generated and minified assets. Preserve behavior and scope.
- Owner readability stays unresolved until the owner reviews the concrete code.
- Do not turn a visual-only website audit into an unsolicited source refactor.

## Make the case for missing business work
A better page cannot answer an open business question. When a finding depends on one the review has no evidence for, help the owner see why it is worth answering.
- Keep both: still report what is wrong on the page. Add the business question beside it.
- Recommend by name: market research for competitors and buyers, pricing for price or packaging, demand validation for unproven demand, business model review or challenge for doubtful viability or conflicting inputs, and venture investigation for a raw idea.
- Persuade: connect what you saw to a buyer's hesitation or an owner's decision. Say what the answer could change and the smallest step to get it, because owners act on reasons they can weigh.
- Be honest: no alarm or promised results. Say it was not in what you reviewed, and that the answer might change nothing.
- Leave the choice open: it blocks nothing and does not change readiness by itself. If the owner declines, do not press unless new evidence appears.
- In the short summary: when a business question could change what the owner does next, put the most important one there with the work by name, why it matters and that it is their choice. Owners may read nothing else.
- Hold back: raise only what could change a buyer's decision. A clear, supported offer needs none.

## Return what to change first
Write the summary for the owner's decision and the findings for whoever makes the fix.
- Lead: with readiness, whether verification is complete or what it lacks, and what most stands between the right customer and choosing the business.
- Short summary: keep it to readiness, verification, the assumed customer, blockers, first changes, the business question and, in a first look, the owner questions. Put what to keep, open questions and how to learn after it, before the findings.
- Assumed customer: say who the review judged for and how sure you are.
- Blockers: show every one, because they cannot wait their turn.
- Main action: put a fault on the way to the main action in the summary, as a blocker or among the first changes, even if one attempt got through. Say where it fails, because every other improvement depends on customers getting through.
- First changes: give up to three beyond the blockers, fewer if fewer matter. For each, say where the customer hesitates, why, what to change and how confident you are.
- Rank: weigh how much a problem affects the choice, how many visitors are likely to meet it and how strong the evidence is. These are judgments, not scores.
- Improvements: ready does not mean finished. Encourage the owner to close the other material problems, before launch where practical. For each, say what customers may lose while it stays open and the smallest fix, because owners act on reasons they can weigh. Promise no results and raise no alarm. The choice stays with the owner, and these do not change readiness. Do not press again without new evidence.
- Replacing the site: when asked, and outside a first look, say whether the evidence points to fixing, redesigning or rebuilding. In the same paragraph, name the unknowns that could change that view, such as editing needs, the live site or current search and enquiry figures. Before anything is replaced, advise securing access and recording current pages, addresses and figures.
- Keep: name what already works, so changes do not damage it.
- Open questions: show what page edits cannot answer.
- Learn: suggest a practical way to tell whether the changes helped, such as asking new enquirers why they chose the business and what nearly stopped them. With little traffic, conversion numbers alone may say little.
- Findings: below the summary, give each material finding a location or state, observation, buyer impact, evidence and practical correction, ordered by consequence. Group systemic issues and link proof. Do not narrate each check.
- Failures: a fake primary conversion or an unsupported central claim remains a failure regardless of unrelated passes. Broken journeys and accessibility barriers still count even when persuasion is strong.
- Repairs: fix reversible defects only when implementation is in scope. Review-only work does not edit the site. Recheck affected views and downstream journeys after repairs.
- Records: record concise findings in existing project records. Stop at the requested outcome.
- Gaps: if another capability would resolve a named gap, recommend it with evidence. The categories are positioning, design, authorized launch and the business work above.

**Wording:** be truthful and growth-minded. Say "not yet" where something can still happen, and keep evidence, warnings and results exact.
