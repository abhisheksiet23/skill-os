# Website

Turn a broad website request into one coherent visitor experience: a credible offer, a buyer story, distinctive expression and a working route to a business outcome. Supply whole-site judgment without imposing a sequence of specialist engagements.

## Establish the site decision

Read company material and adequate existing decisions, then inspect the current site/repository. Determine who arrives, what makes the offer relevant now, what evidence helps them decide and what useful next action the business can support. Include alternatives, fit/exclusions, operating ownership and constraints. Separate a website assignment from changing the business itself; preserve valuable content, URLs, integrations and editing workflows.

Choose the next work from the uncertainty most likely to invalidate the result. An unclear offer needs clarification; an approved design may need implementation; a broken form needs repair. Reuse accepted direction, usable assets and candidate-bound proof while their assumptions still hold; reopen only what the change invalidates. Do not restart positioning or build a fresh prototype for an established, contained correction. If the work spans these concerns, keep their decisions connected rather than handing back separate reports.

## Make the direction concrete

Give each important page a visitor question, a message, supporting proof and an appropriate next step. Develop real copy and a company-specific visual idea together. Inspect a representative responsive journey before extending the pattern across the site; a framework choice, attractive hero or successful build does not establish the experience.

Trace the buyer argument across likely entry pages, not just navigation order. Use a compact working map where the joins are uncertain; it is a diagnostic, not a required page template:

| Argument | Decision to make | Cross-page/operating check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Promise | What useful change can this buyer expect? | Search/campaign wording, page headline and actual service describe the same offer. |
| Proof | Why believe this promise here? | Evidence is close to the claim, attributable and qualified; a linked case supports the relevant outcome. |
| Fit | Who should proceed or choose another route? | Service scope, exclusions and practical constraints survive into detail pages and qualification. |
| Objection | What could reasonably stop this purchase? | Relevant process, risk, comparison or pricing information appears before the decision that needs it. |
| Action | What happens when the visitor acts? | CTA, form, confirmation, receiving operator and follow-up agree on the commitment. |

A referral visitor may already trust the people but need scope and availability; a search visitor may need to establish both relevance and credibility. Let those differences change entry-page depth and links rather than repeating the entire homepage on every page. When a join fails, fix the underlying promise, evidence or operation before polishing its wording. For example, “Book a consultation” requires an actual booking or a clear request-and-confirm process; a generic thank-you cannot settle which one the business offers.

Judge the joins as carefully as the components: does the promise match the service, does proof support that promise, does the CTA set correct expectations, and can the business receive and handle the resulting action? Evaluate buyer clarity, visual craft, accessible interaction and operational readiness together. Distinguish UI feedback, provider acceptance and actual receipt when describing a conversion path.

Use only supportable claims, prices, guarantees, locations and delivery commitments. Separate owner statements, external evidence, assumptions and permission to publish. Conceptual imagery and simulated forms must be recognizable as such; neither supplies documentary or delivery proof.

## Complete at the requested boundary

Keep the site or concrete direction primary. Explain consequential page/journey choices through buyer evidence and the action they support. Use meaningful labels and short lists for decisions, proof and dependencies; use a comparison table when it makes an actual choice easier. Keep the buyer argument and its qualifications intact rather than reducing the website copy to keywords. In existing project records distinguish accepted decisions, the reviewed candidate, live facts and unresolved dependencies. Report what was actually inspected and tested; missing evidence remains a gap.

Honor delegated authority and existing approvals. Pause only dependent work for a genuinely reserved decision. Preparation alone does not authorize publication, purchases or messages to people.

## Optional expertise

For a gap that needs specialist depth, recommend the question and useful evidence: positioning (`website-discover`), narrative and design (`website-create`), implementation (`website-build`), candidate assessment (`website-review`), authorized launch (`website-deliver`), or demand evidence (`business-validate`). These are optional contributions. The agent chooses expertise; this skill neither invokes siblings nor requires their artifacts.
