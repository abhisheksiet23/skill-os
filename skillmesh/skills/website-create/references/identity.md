# Create a brand identity

Use this only when no usable brand exists or the owner has authorized changing it. Keep suitable existing brand elements.

The identity must follow from the company's position and survive real use on the page, not just look good as a logo.

## Terms
- **Identity:** the name, mark, color, type, voice and usage rules that make the company recognizable.
- **Master asset:** the original editable file from which every delivery file is produced.
- **Attribute:** a personality trait chosen for the company, tied to a visible consequence.

## Start from the position
- Source: use the recommended audience, promise, differentiation and what would misrepresent the company. If the position is unsettled, resolve that first.
- Attributes: choose a few. For each, state what it changes in the mark, color, type, imagery and voice. An attribute that changes no choice is an adjective.
- Misrepresentation: list what the identity must not suggest, such as scale, heritage or credentials the company lacks.
- Competitors: note what the category's identities look like, and where this one should differ or deliberately conform.

## Name
- The name is chosen separately. Record the chosen name, its constraints and any unresolved availability checks.
- Do not change the name as part of this work unless the owner authorizes it.

## Mark and logo
- Directions: explore two or three genuinely different directions on the same brief. Changing only color or a font is a variation, not a direction.
- Tests: check each at favicon size, in a mobile header, in one color, and on light and dark backgrounds.
- Fit: check it beside real headline and body text, not alone on a blank page.
- Choice: recommend one with the trade-off and what each rejected direction would have cost.
- Originality: do not copy a distinctive mark. Do not present a generated or stock mark as final without the provenance checks below.

## Color
- Roles: define background, text, accent, interactive, success and error roles. Do not list swatches without roles.
- Contrast: check text and interactive pairs against accessible contrast. Record the approved pairs.
- Restraint: use few colors. Let the accent carry meaning.
- Tinted neutrals: prefer them to pure black and white, and test them on screens and in print where relevant.

## Type
- Fit: choose for voice, reading demands, language coverage and the loading cost.
- Licensing: confirm usable font files and licenses before relying on a face.
- Fallback: define a fallback stack that keeps the layout intact.
- Scale: set hierarchy, size steps and text measure for narrow and wide layouts.

## Voice
- Basis: derive it from the attributes, known buyer circumstances and real copy.
- Pairs: give a few do and do-not pairs taken from actual company wording.
- Range: state how tone shifts between a first impression, an enquiry form, a form error and a commitment.
- Consistency: follow existing project terminology and approved examples.

## Usage rules
- Logo: clear space, minimum size, approved color versions and misuse examples.
- Imagery: the style and the truth rules, for example that conceptual images must not imply real people or projects.
- Components: how color and type apply to buttons, links, forms and emphasis.
- Size: keep the rules proportional. A small site needs a one-page sheet, not a manual.

## Assets and provenance
- Set: vector master, one-color and reversed versions, favicon set, social share image and a text-only fallback.
- Delivery files: produce them from the master. Keep the master separate from optimized files.
- Provenance: record each asset's source, author, license and generation method. Keep private references and rejected attempts out of deliverables.
- Brand assets: once approved, preserve them. Do not regenerate them.

## Limits
- Trademark: this work does not clear a name or mark. Recommend a legal check before relying on it.
- Craft: a final logo may need a professional designer or tool. Say so where the available tools cannot reach the quality needed.
- Evidence: the identity is a reasoned proposal. It is not proven recognition or measured brand effect.
- Critique: inspect the identity in the rendered prototype at narrow, intermediate and wide sizes before presenting. A logo sheet alone does not demonstrate it.
