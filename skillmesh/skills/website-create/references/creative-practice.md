# Content and art direction

## Narrative before a section recipe

Visitors need enough information to recognize relevance, understand the offer, assess fit, trust the company, and progress. The ordering and emphasis depend on entry intent and buying complexity. Do not force every page into hero, feature cards, testimonials, FAQ, CTA.

Draft a page's main proposition and supporting argument before arranging sections. Each section should answer a visitor question or supply necessary evidence. Remove repetitions dressed up as new headings. Use descriptive headings that carry meaning when scanned. Benefits need a mechanism or proof; service detail needs a reason the buyer should care.

Write in the vocabulary buyers understand, retaining precise specialist terms where they help the intended audience. Avoid substituting "innovative," "trusted," "world-class," or "end-to-end" for substance. Read the copy aloud for plausibility and rhythm. Do not turn every sentence into a slogan. Make CTA wording explain the next step and use expectation-setting copy only when operationally true.

For evidence-light companies, show real scope, method, constraints, people, and useful explanations. Clearly hypothetical worked examples can educate; they cannot serve as disguised case studies. Never fill a testimonial carousel with invented praise.

## A creative idea with consequences

Describe the idea in a short statement tied to the business, then demonstrate its consequences for layout, type, imagery, tone, and interaction. For example, a company whose differentiator is making a complex process understandable might emphasize annotated explanations and clear sequencing. The concept must arise from the brief; do not reuse that treatment universally.

Record reference observations: what works, why it is relevant, what would misrepresent this company, and how the proposed design differs. Learn principles from references without copying proprietary text, branded graphics, or a distinctive composition wholesale.

Choose typography for voice, reading demands, usable font files/licensing, and loading cost. Establish hierarchy, text measure, spacing rhythm, alignment, color roles, and image behavior. Let content determine layout; avoid forcing every idea into a card. Repetition can establish coherence, but different page purposes may need different compositions.

For a new direction, record a small set of inspected visual benchmarks and specific qualities the site should meet: typographic confidence, composition, image treatment, detail, and company relevance. Create a representative mobile composition first, including the opening impression and the complete reading journey. Recompose intentionally for larger screens. Do not rely on a large desktop hero to supply all the site's character. Originality can come from photography, typography, illustration, diagrams, or the way the story unfolds; complex effects are not a substitute.

Use motion when it clarifies feedback, sequence, or meaning. It must not delay access to content, obstruct navigation, or make reduced-motion use unpleasant. Decorative complexity must earn its performance and maintenance cost.

## Asset decisions

Record availability, source, rights, intended role, crop/ratio, and factual implications of imagery. For material asset work, use [artwork guidance](artwork.md). Distinguish documentary assets from conceptual illustration. The agent selects tools and additional expertise; this reference does not invoke skills. Generated artwork is optional; authentic photography often carries evidence illustration cannot replace.

Choose an honest fallback when material is missing: licensed conceptual imagery, diagrams, intentional typography, a photography brief, or a reduced claim. Do not present a synthetic office, team, project, or product as the company's real one. Keep private source materials out of deployed assets.

## Representative prototype

Select pages/states that test the distinctive story and the difficult content: long service explanations, proof, comparison, pricing qualifications, or forms as applicable. Use realistic copy lengths. Test mobile reading order and navigation, not just shrinkage of the desktop layout. Prototype form validation, success, and failure visibly; clearly label external calls as simulated unless genuinely integrated.

For substantial visual uncertainty, compare a small number of genuinely different concepts on representative content. Explain the trade-off and recommend one. Do not build complete alternate websites merely to avoid choosing.

## Critique before presenting

- Comprehension: can a new visitor identify the company, offer, intended fit, and next action without explanation? The agent's assessment is a heuristic, not user-research proof.
- Specificity: which parts could belong unchanged to a competitor? Generic utility patterns are fine; the central story and expression need a reason to belong here.
- Credibility: do claims, proof, visuals, and operational promises agree?
- Hierarchy: is the scan path intentional? Are crucial qualifications legible rather than visually buried?
- Composition: inspect type scale, line breaks, text measure, spacing, alignment, image quality/crops, and section transitions in the browser.
- Art direction: compare the complete page to the recorded benchmarks; check that the asset family is coherent, imagery is meaningful, and secondary sections/pages sustain the opening's quality. A beautiful isolated hero does not establish a beautiful website.
- Mobile: inspect content order, navigation, touch affordances, forms, sticky elements, overflow, and the use of limited space.
- Interaction: check focus, keyboard access, reduced motion, contrast, link meaning, validation, and recovery where represented.

For each meaningful weakness, identify the location, visible evidence, cause, and correction. Re-render affected views after fixing. Review mobile first, then intermediate and desktop compositions. Save only enough evidence to substantiate the reviewed direction. If the result still looks generic or underdeveloped against its own direction, revise before presenting it as finished. A numerical self-score does not establish craft.
