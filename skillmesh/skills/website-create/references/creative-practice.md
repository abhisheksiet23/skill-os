# Develop and test a creative argument

Turn company evidence into a page people can read and a visual direction they can recognize. Move between copy, composition and interaction as the candidate exposes weaknesses. Do not finish each in isolation and assemble them at the end.

## Terms
- **Evidence job:** the question or proof a section exists to answer for the visitor.
- **Creative idea:** a stated link to the business plus the layout, type, imagery, tone and interaction choices that follow from it. A concept that explains no concrete choice is an adjective, not art direction.
- **Benchmark:** an inspected visual reference with the specific qualities the site should meet.

## Narrative before a section recipe
Visitors need enough information to recognize relevance, understand the offer, assess fit, trust the company and progress. Order and emphasis depend on entry intent and buying complexity.
- Recipe: do not force every page into hero, feature cards, testimonials, FAQ, call to action.
- Start: begin with the proposition the visitor must understand and the argument that makes it credible.
- Target queries: name the searches and questions that bring the visitor, where known. Make the page's opening answer the main one.
- Sections: give each section an evidence job, then arrange the reading journey. Remove sections that restate the proposition under another heading.
- Headings: let them carry meaning when scanned.
- Substance: connect benefits to mechanisms or proof. Explain why service detail matters to this buyer.
- Filler: if a layout needs generic filler to look complete, revisit the argument.

## Copy and voice
Write in the buyer's vocabulary.
- Terms: keep precise specialist terms where they help the intended audience.
- Empty words: do not substitute "innovative", "trusted", "world-class" or "end-to-end" for substance.
- Rhythm: check the copy for natural phrasing and plausibility. Do not turn every sentence into a slogan.
- Calls to action: front-load the meaningful outcome or action, and make each understandable on its own. Add expectation-setting copy only when it is operationally true.
- Tone: stay consistent in voice. Be efficient for routine steps, explanatory for a first visit, calm for errors and explicit for consequential choices.
- Source of voice: follow project terminology, house style and approved examples. Derive wording from this audience and offer, not a universal voice.
- Contrasts: use project-specific contrasts to clarify voice. Do not require a new brand exercise.
- New voice: use known buyer circumstances and company values where they resolve a writing choice. Keep proposals provisional and surface conflicts.
- Forms and confirmations: preserve actual requirements, submission status and available recovery. Do not invent causes, receipt guarantees or response times.
- Editing: edit for purpose, brevity, natural language and clarity. Set no fixed word limits and do not lose qualifications.
- Thin evidence: for evidence-light companies, show real scope, method, constraints, people and useful explanations.
- Hypotheticals: clearly labeled worked examples can educate. They cannot serve as disguised case studies.
- Praise: never fill a testimonial carousel with invented praise.

## Write findable, quotable pages
Write each page so a visitor, a search engine and an AI assistant can each take the same accurate meaning from it.
- Opening: answer the page's question in plain, specific terms before elaborating.
- Headings: state the topic. Do not use a clever line a reader cannot scan.
- Passages: make each self-contained, with its qualification beside the claim, so an excerpt stays accurate.
- Facts as text: state scope, locations, credentials, process and any approved price ranges as page text, not only in images, sliders or scripts.
- Questions: add an FAQ only for questions buyers actually ask. Do not invent questions to add markup.
- Duplicates: do not produce near-identical pages for different keywords or locations.
- Stuffing: do not repeat keywords for their own sake. Check that each repeated term earns its place.
- Titles and descriptions: draft each page's title and description as part of the copy.
- Claims: never promise rankings, citations or traffic.

## Give the creative idea consequences
State the idea's connection to the business, then show what changes in layout, type, imagery, tone and interaction because of it. A company that makes complexity understandable might use annotations and clear sequencing. That is an example of a consequence, not a reusable house style.

References:
- Record: what works, why it is relevant, what would misrepresent this company and how the proposed design differs.
- Learn principles. Do not copy proprietary text, branded graphics or a distinctive composition wholesale.

Visual system:
- Typography: choose for voice, reading demands, usable font files and licensing, and loading cost.
- Structure: establish hierarchy, text measure, spacing rhythm, alignment, color roles and image behavior.
- Layout: let content determine it. Do not force every idea into a card. Repetition builds coherence, but different page purposes may need different compositions.

New direction:
- Benchmarks: record a small set of inspected visual benchmarks. Name the qualities the site should meet: typographic confidence, composition, image treatment, detail and company relevance.
- Mobile first: create a representative mobile composition first, covering the opening impression and the complete reading journey. Recompose intentionally for larger screens.
- Desktop hero: do not rely on a large desktop hero to supply the site's character.
- Originality: it can come from photography, typography, illustration, diagrams or how the story unfolds. Complex effects are not a substitute.

Motion:
- Use it when it clarifies feedback, sequence or meaning.
- It must not delay access to content, obstruct navigation or make reduced-motion use unpleasant.
- Decorative complexity must earn its performance and maintenance cost.

## Compare directions through their consequences
Compare only when a new direction has a real unresolved choice. Skip it when accepted direction already settles the choice.
- Basis: compare concepts on the same buyer problem and evidence.
- Test: a distinct direction changes what the visitor notices, how the argument unfolds and what assets are needed.
- Recommendation: base it on buyer relevance, supported proof, composition at small sizes and realistic production and maintenance cost. Explain the trade-off and recommend one.
- Limit: do not build complete alternate websites to avoid choosing.
- Missing assets: an unavailable documentary asset may change the direction. It is not a reason to synthesize fake evidence.
- Output: the comparison is a decision aid. Keep the substantial page narrative and do not replace website copy with it.

### Difficult-page test
Run it on the page most likely to break the direction: for example a service page with exclusions, a long case study, a comparison or a mobile enquiry flow.
- Carry through: one difficult passage, one proof unit with its qualification, one image and crop, and one consequential interaction.
- Inspect: whether the character survives real content, and whether reading order keeps each qualification beside its claim.
- Fix first: if the concept works only with a short hero headline, revise it before multiplying pages.

## Asset decisions
- Substantial imagery: use [artwork guidance](artwork.md) to plan and record each asset.
- Smaller needs: record each asset's source, rights and factual implications.
- Fallbacks when material is missing: licensed conceptual imagery, diagrams, intentional typography, a photography brief or a reduced claim.
- Never present a synthetic office, team, project or product as the company's real one. Generated artwork is optional and cannot replace documentary evidence.
- Keep private source materials out of deployed assets.

## Representative prototype
Choose the smallest set of pages and states that could reveal a wrong direction.
- Content: include the distinctive story and difficult content, such as long service explanations, proof, comparisons, pricing qualifications or forms. A favorable short-copy homepage alone is a weak test of a multi-page design.
- Forms: show validation, success and failure visibly. Label external calls as simulated unless genuinely integrated.
- Order: render the sample before extending shared layouts. Correct weaknesses in argument, composition and interaction first. Then reuse the working pattern where page purposes match.
- Expansion: widen the sample when a different page purpose or state introduces a new risk. An inspected homepage does not cover a complex detail page by implication.

## Critique before presenting
Use these lenses to locate a weakness, not to award a reassuring score.
- Comprehension: can a new visitor identify the company, offer, intended fit and next action without explanation? The agent's assessment is a heuristic, not user-research proof.
- Specificity: which parts could belong unchanged to a competitor? Generic utility patterns are fine. The central story and its expression need a reason to belong here.
- Credibility: do claims, proof, visuals and operational promises agree?
- Hierarchy: is the scan path intentional? Are crucial qualifications legible rather than visually buried?
- Composition: inspect type scale, line breaks, text measure, spacing, alignment, image quality and crops, and section transitions in the browser.
- Art direction: compare the complete page to the recorded benchmarks. Check that the asset family is coherent, imagery is meaningful and secondary sections and pages sustain the opening's quality. A beautiful isolated hero does not establish a beautiful website.
- Mobile: inspect content order, navigation, touch affordances, forms, sticky elements, overflow and use of limited space.
- Interaction: check focus, keyboard access, reduced motion, contrast, link meaning, validation and recovery where represented.

For each meaningful finding:
- Identify the visible evidence, the likely cause and the correction.
- Fix it, then re-render the affected view and any other composition sharing the change.
- Review mobile first, then intermediate and desktop layouts.

Judge completion against the creative idea and the complete reading journey. If the opening is strong but secondary sections are generic, the direction is not yet demonstrated. Retain enough inspected evidence to show the result and its limits. A numerical self-score cannot establish craft or measured customer response.
