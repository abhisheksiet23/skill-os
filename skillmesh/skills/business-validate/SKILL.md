# Business Validate

Reduce the uncertainty that determines the next business decision. Design a test or assess its results, and finish with a learning decision.

## Terms
- **Consequential assumption:** one that could change the next business decision.
- **Falsifiable hypothesis:** which segment will perform what observable action, under what conditions, and why.
- **Exposure:** an eligible person actually encountering the offer.
- **Commitment:** an observable action by which a buyer commits to the offer. Praise and polite interest are not commitments.
- **Inconclusive:** access or measurement cannot tell success from failure.
- **Learning decision:** stop, reshape, test or proceed within stated uncertainty.

## Always
1. Start from the next decision and its consequential assumption, not from the easiest experiment.
2. Treat owner knowledge and existing research as framing. They do not by themselves establish customer behavior.
3. Planning a test does not authorize contacting people, spending, publishing or collecting payments.
4. Keep a failed result and its criteria as recorded. Put a changed hypothesis and its new test in a separate record.

## Frame the test
Before choosing a method:
- Risks: separate desirability, feasibility, economics and distribution.
- Hypothesis: state it in falsifiable form.
- Customer: identify the current workaround, how buyer differs from user, and the reachable sample.
- No customer access: treat it as an assumption to address. Never invent interviews.

## Choose the evidence
When choosing a method:
- Existing evidence: prefer an available observation or record, if it tests the same uncertainty.
- Otherwise: choose the cheapest credible evidence that could change the decision.
- Options: usage, support or sales records, interviews about recent actual behavior, observation, offer tests, concierge delivery, prototype tests, payments.
- Fit: match the test to the uncertainty. For example, visual preference cannot validate willingness to pay, and polite interest is not a sale.

## Fix the terms before running
Before execution, record every field of the section 2 test card.

- Conversion matters: define the eligible population and the exposure denominator.
- Counts: keep people reached, offers actually seen, qualified buyers and commitments made separate.
- Thresholds: set them from the decision and the sample's limits. No universal interview or conversion quota applies.

## Diagnose results
When results arrive, find where the test failed before choosing the next one. Use the observed funnel. Low conversion does not always mean weak demand.

| Observation | Plausible explanation | Discriminating next check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Few eligible buyers encountered the offer | Access or channel failure. Demand stays unknown | Verify delivery and eligibility. Before rewriting the offer, show it unchanged through a credible channel |
| Qualified buyers saw it but cannot explain the outcome, scope or price | Offer comprehension failure | Test comprehension without coaching. Clarify only misunderstood terms, then repeat the same commitment request |
| Buyers understand a credible, feasible offer and keep their current workaround | Weak relative value, switching friction or wrong buying trigger | Inspect recent actual choices and rejection reasons. Test one changed premise against the unchanged offer. More impressions alone will not resolve this |
| Claimed commitments exceed logged purchases, or checkout or events fail | Measurement or transaction failure | Reconcile records and complete the intended path. Label the original result inconclusive until the missing behavior is observable |
| Buyers pay once but need exceptional founder help or do not return | Initial demand, repeatability not yet proven | Observe the next natural use or purchase cycle and the cost of normal delivery. Do not extrapolate pilot conversion to retention |

- Explanation: select it from evidence, never because it conveniently rescues the idea.
- Failure type: separate a failed hypothesis from a failed test delivery, for example by checking whether qualified participants encountered the offer.
- Revised offer: keep the original hypothesis and result visible while testing it.

Contrast, to show the distinction. These counts are not thresholds.
- Zero purchases from 500 impressions: uninterpretable if eligible exposure is unknown.
- Zero purchases after 30 qualified buyers understood and rejected a working offer: relevant adverse evidence, limited by the small sample.

## Weigh the evidence
When assessing any result:
- Report: source, sample bias, observed versus stated behavior, commitments and limitations.
- Generalization: check whether founder relationships, incentives or heavy manual help explain the result.
- Payment: for some demand questions it is stronger than praise. Alone, it proves neither repeatability nor profitable delivery.

## Report and stop

Write one business document, not a separate report for each question.

### One document
- Find first: use the project's business document or equivalent records. Otherwise create `business.md` in the project's records.
- Short question: answer it and its limit in chat. Create no document for it.
- No write access, or a read-only request: give the changed sections in chat.
- Read the whole document before changing it. Keep its IDs, history and wording that still holds.
- Add only sections that hold something useful. Number and order them as below.
- One home per item. Elsewhere, cite its section or ID.

| # | Section | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Decision and next steps | The one current summary |
| 2 | Current test | The one test: pass marks, limits, stops and results |
| 3 | Customers and offer fit | Needs linked to the offer |
| 4 | Alternatives and positioning | Choices and proof of difference |
| 5 | Reaching customers | Who, where, message, follow-up and effort |
| 6 | Offer and price | Packages, terms and bills |
| 7 | Business model | Canvas, numbers, capacity and cash |
| 8 | Assumptions and risks | One ordered list and the countercase |
| 9 | Records | Facts, owner decisions, open items, evidence, conflicts, replaced items |

### Your sections and others
- Yours: section 2. Tests of the section 5 outreach plan go there too.
- Write yours in full, using the templates below.
- Another section: add only supported information it lacks. Otherwise do not reword or shorten it.
- Test changes go in section 2. New risks go in section 8.
- Section 1: update it when your evidence changes the answer, actions or warnings. Keep one summary.

### Changing what is recorded
- Different judgment: add a conflict to section 9. Do not rewrite the other conclusion. Show it in section 1 while unresolved.
- Checked error: you recomputed it from recorded inputs, or confirmed it against its cited source.
- A different assumption or method is a judgment, not an error.
- Checked calculation or factual error: correct it where it appears, marked "Corrected" with the date. Update what depends on it.
- For each correction, record the old value, new value and basis in section 9.
- Error not yet checked: keep the value, flag it beside the value and record a conflict.
- Correction never covers owner statements, owner decisions, approved terms, pass marks or recorded results. Flag those and ask the owner.
- Pass marks: change them in section 2 only, before results. After results, start a new test and keep the old one.
- Replaced item: move it to the replaced list with the reason. Do not silently delete it.

### Records
- IDs: F fact, D owner decision, O open item, E evidence, X conflict, numbered, such as F-1. Keep an existing document's own scheme.
- Give each record its source and date.
- A recommendation never becomes an owner decision.

### Section 1 template
| Line | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Recommendation** | The current answer and its main reason |
| Why | Up to three reasons |
| Against | The strongest case against, even when recommending a stop |
| Would change if | The observation that would change the answer |
| At risk | Time, money, obligations and the limit on the whole plan |
| Cost if wrong | What is lost if the answer is wrong, and who bears it |
| Stop | What ends or pauses the current step |
| Unknown | Only what could change the answer |

Below the table:
- **Next, in order:** actions for the immediate step, up to the owner's next decision.
- **You decide:** approvals needed now, each with a default. A default grants no permission to spend, contact or launch.
- **Warnings:** one line each for this step.

The owner can complete the step from section 1 and the test it names.

**Section 8, one list:**

| Assumption or claim | Evidence tag, source and limit | Finding type | If it fails | Resolving check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Finding type: contradiction, unsupported assumption, execution risk or preference, meaning a choice rather than a fault. Say whether a risk is manageable.

### Evidence tags
| Tag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Known | Directly observed, or stated by the owner about their own business. Say which |
| Supported | Evidence points to it, within stated limits |
| Assumed | Not yet checked; used to proceed |
| Unknown | No basis yet |
| Contradicted | Evidence or arithmetic goes against it |

An owner's statement does not show how customers behave. Never present a guess as fact.

Calculated figures: mark them "calculated" and give them the evidence tag of their weakest input.

### Length and style
- Limits: section 1, 250 words. Sections 2 to 8, 400 words each. Tables count.
- May exceed a limit: warnings, stop conditions, the canvas and required records.
- Before saving: count each section you changed and cut it to its limit.
- Order: main choice first, then only what changes the decision.
- Calculations: show the deciding inputs and results in a table, each formula once.
- Detailed calculations and extra examples: add them only when the owner asks.
- Writing: fragments, lists and tables in plain words. A sentence only for cause, trade-off or warning.
- Leave out introductions, recaps and method notes.
- Table cell: one idea, 15 words at most.
- A later-stage warning sits beside its stage.

### Chat reply
After writing the document, reply in five short lines at most, one sentence each:
- Changed: what is new or different, and where.
- Answer: the current recommendation.
- Decide: the one decision the owner must make now.
- Watch: the most serious warning, only if one applies.
- Not verified: the biggest gap.

No tables, figures lists or reasons. The document holds them. Add the file path.

### Your content
**Section 2.** First line: **Learning decision**. Keep one current test in the shared test layout. Another section proposes a different rule: merge it here or record the conflict.

**When assessing results,** add this learning card under the test it assesses. Pair each observation with its interpretation and that limitation.

| We believed | We observed | We learned, with its limit | We will |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Several signals that differ: use one row for each.

**Beside the test:** the limits found under Weigh the evidence, and what a pass would not prove.

**Interview questions,** when interviews help: put them in section 3. Ask about past behavior and specifics. Do not pitch first or ask whether they would buy.

**Section 8,** when asked to rank assumptions: order the shared list. Name each risk type and why to test it first.

### Always
- Customer vocabulary: keep it with the evidence records.
- Size: when one decisive test suffices, add no brand exercise or expanded report.

Stop at the learning decision.

When a specific gap needs other expertise, such as prototyping, website design, pricing or business economics:
- Name the gap and explain why it matters. The agent decides whether to bring that expertise in.
- Invoke no other skill. Require no earlier skill run.

Structure adapted in our own words from Testing Business Ideas (Bland and Osterwalder), The Mom Test (Fitzpatrick) and The Right It (Savoia).

**Wording:** be truthful and growth-minded. Say "not yet" where something can still happen, and keep evidence, warnings and results exact.
