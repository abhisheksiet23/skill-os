# Business Validate

Reduce the uncertainty that determines the next business decision. Start with that decision and the consequential assumption, not the easiest experiment. Separate desirability, feasibility, economics and distribution risks. Existing owner knowledge and research help frame a test but do not automatically establish customer behavior.

## Match evidence to the hypothesis

State a falsifiable hypothesis: which segment will perform what observable action, under what conditions, and why. Identify the current workaround, buyer/user distinction and reachable sample. Lack of customer access is an assumption to address, not permission to invent interviews.

Prefer an available observation or record before creating a new experiment, provided it tests the same uncertainty. Choose the cheapest credible evidence that could change the decision: existing usage/support/sales records, interviews about recent actual behavior, observation, offer tests, concierge delivery, prototype tests or payments. Match the test to uncertainty; visual preference cannot validate willingness to pay, and polite interest is not a sale.

## Make the test interpretable before running it

Before execution, record hypothesis, participants/recruitment, method, success/failure signal, threshold, time/cost bound and next action. Define the eligible population and exposure denominator where conversion matters; distinguish people reached, offers actually seen, qualified buyers and commitments made. Choose thresholds from the decision and sample limitations, not universal interview or conversion quotas. Include an inconclusive outcome when access or measurement cannot discriminate success from failure. Distinguish planning from authorization to contact people, spend, publish or collect payments.

## Interpret results without changing the question

Assess results honestly: source, sample bias, observed versus stated behavior, commitments and limitations. Separate failure of the hypothesis from a failed test delivery, using evidence such as whether qualified participants actually encountered the offer. Founder relationships, incentives and heavy manual assistance can explain a result that will not generalize. Payment is stronger than praise for some demand questions but does not alone prove repeatability or profitable delivery. Do not move the goalposts after failure; record a changed hypothesis and new test separately.

Lead with **Learning decision**: stop, reshape, test or proceed within stated uncertainty. Represent a proposed test as concise labeled facts: hypothesis; eligible audience/exposure; action measured; threshold; limit; next decision. For results, pair observation with interpretation and its limitation, using a small table if several signals differ. Distinguish approved decisions and assumptions. Preserve useful evidence and customer vocabulary in existing records; avoid a brand exercise or an expanded report when one decisive test suffices.

Stop at the learning decision. Optionally recommend `app-prototype`, `website-create`, `business-pricing` or `business-model` for a specific gap. Explain the need; the agent decides. Do not invoke skills or require prior invocations.
