# Business Validate

Reduce the uncertainty that determines the next business decision. Design a test or assess its results, and finish with a learning decision.

## Terms
- **Consequential assumption:** one that could change the next business decision.
- **Falsifiable hypothesis:** which segment will perform what observable action, under what conditions, and why.
- **Exposure:** an eligible person actually encountering the offer.
- **Commitment:** an observable action by which a buyer commits to the offer. Praise and polite interest are not commitments.
- **Inconclusive:** access or measurement cannot tell success from failure.
- **Learning decision:** stop, reshape, test or proceed within stated uncertainty.

## Always
1. Start from the next decision and its consequential assumption, not from the easiest experiment.
2. Treat owner knowledge and existing research as framing. They do not by themselves establish customer behavior.
3. Planning a test does not authorize contacting people, spending, publishing or collecting payments.
4. Keep a failed result and its criteria as recorded. Put a changed hypothesis and its new test in a separate record.

## Frame the test
Before choosing a method:
- Risks: separate desirability, feasibility, economics and distribution.
- Hypothesis: state it in falsifiable form.
- Customer: identify the current workaround, how buyer differs from user, and the reachable sample.
- No customer access: treat it as an assumption to address. Never invent interviews.

## Choose the evidence
When choosing a method:
- Existing evidence: prefer an available observation or record, if it tests the same uncertainty.
- Otherwise: choose the cheapest credible evidence that could change the decision.
- Options: usage, support or sales records, interviews about recent actual behavior, observation, offer tests, concierge delivery, prototype tests, payments.
- Fit: match the test to the uncertainty. For example, visual preference cannot validate willingness to pay, and polite interest is not a sale.

## Fix the terms before running
Before execution, record:

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Hypothesis | The falsifiable statement |
| Participants | Who they are and how they are recruited |
| Method | How the evidence is gathered |
| Signal | What counts as success and as failure |
| Threshold | The level that separates them |
| Bound | Time and cost limit |
| Next action | What follows from the result |

- Conversion matters: define the eligible population and the exposure denominator.
- Counts: keep people reached, offers actually seen, qualified buyers and commitments made separate.
- Thresholds: set them from the decision and the sample's limits. No universal interview or conversion quota applies.
- Inconclusive: include this outcome for when access or measurement cannot tell success from failure.

## Diagnose results
When results arrive, find where the test failed before choosing the next one. Use the observed funnel. Low conversion does not always mean weak demand.

| Observation | Plausible explanation | Discriminating next check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Few eligible buyers encountered the offer | Access or channel failure. Demand stays unknown | Verify delivery and eligibility. Before rewriting the offer, show it unchanged through a credible channel |
| Qualified buyers saw it but cannot explain the outcome, scope or price | Offer comprehension failure | Test comprehension without coaching. Clarify only misunderstood terms, then repeat the same commitment request |
| Buyers understand a credible, feasible offer and keep their current workaround | Weak relative value, switching friction or wrong buying trigger | Inspect recent actual choices and rejection reasons. Test one changed premise against the unchanged offer. More impressions alone will not resolve this |
| Claimed commitments exceed logged purchases, or checkout or events fail | Measurement or transaction failure | Reconcile records and complete the intended path. Label the original result inconclusive until the missing behavior is observable |
| Buyers pay once but need exceptional founder help or do not return | Initial demand, repeatability unproven | Observe the next natural use or purchase cycle and the cost of normal delivery. Do not extrapolate pilot conversion to retention |

- Explanation: select it from evidence, never because it conveniently rescues the idea.
- Failure type: separate a failed hypothesis from a failed test delivery, for example by checking whether qualified participants encountered the offer.
- Revised offer: keep the original hypothesis and result visible while testing it.

Contrast, to show the distinction. These counts are not thresholds.
- Zero purchases from 500 impressions: uninterpretable if eligible exposure is unknown.
- Zero purchases after 30 qualified buyers understood and rejected a working offer: relevant adverse evidence, limited by the small sample.

## Weigh the evidence
When assessing any result:
- Report: source, sample bias, observed versus stated behavior, commitments and limitations.
- Generalization: check whether founder relationships, incentives or heavy manual help explain the result.
- Payment: for some demand questions it is stronger than praise. Alone, it proves neither repeatability nor profitable delivery.

## Report and stop

Use this structure for a substantial result. A short answer needs only the answer and its limit.

### Hard limits
- Whole result: 600 words at most, tables included. This is a limit, not a target.
- Three parts only: decision card, core table, closing table or list.
- Decision card: 200 words at most. Each line: three fragments at most, 12 words each.
- Core table: six rows at most. A template with more standard fields keeps every field. Closing part: four rows at most.
- Assumptions: one line under the table that uses them. No separate calculation walkthrough.
- Say each fact once across all three parts.
- One idea per table cell. Do not pack several rules or branches into a cell.
- Only warnings and stop conditions may push the result past 600 words.
- Too much to fit: keep what changes the decision. Drop the rest.

### Writing style
- Fragments and keywords. No introduction, recap, headline sentence or method notes.
- Short plain words a business owner understands. No unexplained shorthand.
- Evidence tags: Known, Supported, Assumed, Unknown, Contradicted.
- Unknown cell: write "Unknown". Never present a guess as fact.

### Part 1. Decision card
Always first. It covers only the immediate step, up to the owner's next decision. Write it as a two-column table, Line and Content, never as labelled paragraphs.

| Line | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Learning decision** | Stop, reshape, test or proceed within stated uncertainty |
| Why | Up to three reasons |
| Against | The strongest case against your answer, even when you recommend stopping |
| Would change if | The observation that would change the answer, and what it would change |
| Next | Ordered actions in this step |
| Stop | What ends or pauses this step |
| At risk | Time, money, obligations, and the limit on the whole plan |
| Cost if wrong | What is lost if this answer is wrong, and who bears it |
| You decide | Approvals needed now, each with a default. A default grants no permission to spend, contact or launch |
| Warnings | One line each, for this step. Later-stage warnings sit beside their stage below |
| Unknown | Only what could change the answer |

The owner can complete the step from this card. Explain any check it names.

### Part 2. Test card
| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| We believe | Falsifiable hypothesis |
| Who | Eligible audience, recruitment, exposure |
| We will | Method and offer |
| We measure | Observable action and counts kept separate |
| Right if | Pass threshold |
| Wrong if | Fail threshold |
| Inconclusive if | When access or measurement cannot tell |
| Bound | Time and cost |
| Then | Action for each result |

When assessing results, replace the test card with this learning card. Pair each observation with its interpretation and that limitation.

| We believed | We observed | We learned, with its limit | We will |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Several signals that differ: use one row for each.

### Part 3. Limits of this evidence
Short list: bias, stated versus observed, what a pass would not prove.

### Detail on request
End with one line naming what the owner can ask for. Produce a block below only when asked.

#### Assumption under test
| Assumption | Risk type | Evidence today and tag | Why test it first |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

#### Conversation questions
Only when interviews help. Ask about past behavior and specifics. Do not pitch first or ask whether they would buy.

#### Learning card
| We believed | We observed | We learned, with its limit | We will |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

### Always
- Status: distinguish approved decisions from assumptions.
- Records: preserve useful evidence and customer vocabulary in existing project records.
- Size: when one decisive test suffices, add no brand exercise or expanded report.

Stop at the learning decision.

When a specific gap needs other expertise, such as prototyping, website design, pricing or business economics:
- Name the gap and explain why it matters. The agent decides whether to bring that expertise in.
- Invoke no other skill. Require no earlier skill run.

Structure adapted in our own words from Testing Business Ideas (Bland and Osterwalder), The Mom Test (Fitzpatrick) and The Right It (Savoia).
