# Naming Architect

Develop names that fit the audience, purpose and actual use. Treat this framework as practical synthesis: the source ledger mentioned in the owner's original attachment was not supplied, so no book/blog grounding is established.

## Establish the decision

Use the available brief and ask only for missing information that could change the recommendation. Identify what is being named, audience and markets/languages, desired associations, prohibited terms and real usage contexts. Separate nonnegotiable constraints from preferences and unknowns. Admired/disliked names can clarify taste when useful; do not require a questionnaire.

Check the naming architecture: standalone brand, endorsed product, feature within an existing product, series title or internal label? Respect existing vocabulary, sibling names and parent-brand conventions. A feature often needs discoverability within its parent product more than independent brand distinctiveness. Determine whether a separate domain or handle is actually needed; public visibility alone does not imply one.

For renaming, establish the problem with the current name and include keeping it as an option. Weigh any improvement against recognition, customer confusion, search/discovery continuity, documentation, URLs, integrations, support and transition costs. Preserve stable technical identifiers where appropriate; proposing a new display name does not authorize changing them. Recommend retaining the current name when the evidence does not justify disruption. Identify a staged transition or compatibility need without expanding a naming task into implementation.

## Explore and compare

For generation, explore distinct conceptual directions before polishing variants: descriptive function, audience benefit, metaphor, meaningful compounds, coined forms or relevant community vocabulary. Choose directions and candidate counts to suit the brief. For comparison/critique, start with the supplied names; generate alternatives only when useful or requested. Honor explicit counts and formats.

For benefit-led directions, connect what the product does to the practical improvement and then the feeling or aspiration it enables. Use that last association to explore metaphors beyond category vocabulary; check that the name still fits actual use rather than promising an unsupported outcome.

Avoid defaulting to English two-word compounds, short invented words or fashionable suffixes. Explore forms appropriate to the audience, including phrases, local-language words or established series conventions. Never invent etymology or cultural meaning. Borrowed-language candidates need actual language evidence before endorsement.

Prune against the brief. Use realistic spoken/written examples to expose likely misunderstanding, spelling-after-hearing problems, abbreviations, awkward word joins or unintended promises. Consider negative associations, category similarity, future scope and the fit alongside parent/sibling names. Distinguish perceived memorability/distinctiveness from tested recall or researched market separation.

Default to a qualitative comparison: strongest fit, main weakness, decisive trade-off and what remains unknown. Numerical scoring is optional when requested or when an explicit decision process genuinely benefits. Explain criteria, weights and their judgmental nature; do not present small score differences as measured superiority. Mark unsupported criteria unknown, disclose any exclusions or normalization, and check whether reasonable weight changes reverse the recommendation. Never use a total to cancel a violated constraint or confirmed disqualifying conflict.

## Match checks to consequences

Small internal naming tasks need a concise answer with a recommendation; no forced research, scoring or territory report. Larger public brand decisions may justify competitor, linguistic, handle and preliminary trademark checks. Missing live tools limit verification, not useful creative work. Never infer availability from absent search results or treat preliminary screening as legal clearance.

For a new standalone public brand, company or product, include live domain screening of the shortlist by default; do not wait for the user to ask separately. Treat a separate domain as needed unless the brief establishes an existing-domain home or explicitly excludes research. When a name needs a separate domain or the user requests a domain check, read [domain screening](references/domain-screening.md) and perform the checks before the final recommendation. A future checklist or offer to check later does not complete this work. Skip standalone domain research for a feature under an existing brand/domain unless there is a concrete reason. An existing domain does not remove relevant competitor, legal or audience risks.

Use available read-only tools to obtain registration evidence: a registrar/registry tool, browser, or direct RDAP request through an HTTP-capable tool. The absence of a dedicated domain plugin is not evidence that live lookup is unavailable. If a lookup fails, try a suitable independent route where accessible, then report the attempted source and actual failure. If the environment explicitly has no network tools, state that limit without pretending an attempt occurred. Keep unresolved recommendations provisional; an unknown domain cannot satisfy a required availability constraint.

For consequential finalists, propose a small audience test tied to uncertainty: show names in realistic context for comprehension; say them aloud for spelling; test unaided recall after a delay; ask target-language speakers about interpretation and negative associations. Compare candidates under consistent conditions and vary presentation order. Separate preference from comprehension and observed behavior; do not treat an agent's linguistic impression, one enthusiastic response or a proposed test as completed validation. User authorization is needed before contacting people.

## Recommend and iterate

Lead with the recommendation and deciding reason, then give only the shortlist, usage examples, trade-offs and evidence needed to assess it. Include assumptions or open checks that could reverse the choice. No mandatory seven-part report or fixed shortlist size. For high-consequence choices label provisional recommendations clearly and identify the next useful check. A recommendation to keep the current name is a valid outcome.

Learn from rejection reasons and preserve constraints rather than producing cosmetic variations. Keep project-specific shortlists and accepted decisions in the owning project. [Worked examples](references/examples.md) illustrate trade-offs; do not reuse their candidates as evidence of general naming quality.

[Research and attribution](references/research-and-attribution.md) records selectively adapted MIT-licensed ideas and methods deliberately not adopted; read when source provenance or further borrowing matters.

This skill does not provide legal clearance, guaranteed availability or validated consumer research. Do not claim source access, language expertise or live checks without evidence. Naming work does not authorize purchases, registration, outreach, publication or migration. Stop when the requested naming decision is supported.
