# Venture Investigate

Determine whether an idea merits the owner's next commitment of time, money or attention. Finish with a recommendation and the commitment it warrants.

## Terms
- **Next commitment:** the time, money or attention the owner would put in next.
- **Countercase:** the strongest evidence-consistent argument against pursuing the idea.
- **Lens:** a way of finding the next decisive question. It is not a stage or a report section.

## Always
1. Put truth and useful learning ahead of making the idea look successful or completing a framework. A polished pitch does not justify pursuit.
2. Keep recommendations distinct from owner decisions.
3. Imply no product-market fit or investor readiness without proof.
4. No generic score grants permission to spend or launch.
5. Keep company facts in the project's existing records.

## Frame the decision and exposure
Work iteratively with the owner. At the start:
- Idea: frame the customer problem, proposed solution and insight.
- Buyers: distinguish user, payer, beneficiary, current alternative and buying trigger.
- Ambition and constraints: clarify them. A sustainable business and a venture-scale investment are different outcomes.
- Commitment: identify the next one, its reversibility and the uncertainty that could make it wasteful.
- Founder: consider access, capabilities, capital and appetite early. A gap that can be learned or filled by a partner is not an automatic rejection.
- Customer access: treat it as a testable assumption.

## Investigate in order of decision value
- First: investigate the claim most likely to invalidate the next commitment.
- Evidence: start with the cheapest that could falsify the most consequential claim.
- Order follows the case. A costly delivery model may matter before market sizing. An obvious feasibility constraint may matter before interviews.
- Coverage: do not complete every lens for its own sake.
- Reassess after each meaningful finding.

To locate important gaps or choose a bounded commitment, read [investigation lenses and commitment contrasts](references/investigation.md). The lenses are not mandatory stages.

## Keep claims honest
Mark each consequential claim as known, supported, assumed, unknown or contradicted. Give its source, scope and limitations.
- Demand: commitment, payment and repeat use usually support it more strongly than praise or competitor presence. Strength still depends on relevance and sampling.
- Public offers: they do not establish competitor profitability.
- Simulated economics: they are not evidence of sales.
- Conflicting evidence: before averaging it into a confidence label, inspect differences in segment, buying conditions, period and method.
- Balance: preserve the strongest countercase as well as the supporting findings.

## Recommend the next commitment

Use this structure for a substantial result. A short answer needs only the answer and its limit.

### Hard limits
- Whole result: 600 words at most, tables included. This is a limit, not a target.
- Three parts only: decision card, core table, closing table or list.
- Decision card: 200 words at most. Each line: three fragments at most, 12 words each.
- Core table: six rows at most. A template with more standard fields keeps every field. Closing part: four rows at most.
- Assumptions: one line under the table that uses them. No separate calculation walkthrough.
- Say each fact once across all three parts.
- One idea per table cell. Do not pack several rules or branches into a cell.
- Only warnings and stop conditions may push the result past 600 words.
- Too much to fit: keep what changes the decision. Drop the rest.

### Writing style
- Fragments and keywords. No introduction, recap, headline sentence or method notes.
- Short plain words a business owner understands. No unexplained shorthand.
- Evidence tags: Known, Supported, Assumed, Unknown, Contradicted.
- Unknown cell: write "Unknown". Never present a guess as fact.

### Part 1. Decision card
Always first. It covers only the immediate step, up to the owner's next decision. Write it as a two-column table, Line and Content, never as labelled paragraphs.

| Line | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Recommendation** | Reject, park, reshape, test, build or scale, with the next commitment it warrants |
| Why | Up to three reasons |
| Against | The strongest case against your answer, even when you recommend stopping |
| Would change if | The observation that would change the answer, and what it would change |
| Next | Ordered actions in this step |
| Stop | What ends or pauses this step |
| At risk | Time, money, obligations, and the limit on the whole plan |
| Cost if wrong | What is lost if this answer is wrong, and who bears it |
| You decide | Approvals needed now, each with a default. A default grants no permission to spend, contact or launch |
| Warnings | One line each, for this step. Later-stage warnings sit beside their stage below |
| Unknown | Only what could change the answer |

The owner can complete the step from this card. Explain any check it names.

### Part 2. Riskiest assumptions
| Assumption | If false | Evidence tag, source and limit | Test or next evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Order by consequence and weakness of evidence. Include desirability, feasibility, economics and distribution where they matter.

### Part 3. Next commitment
| Step | Limit | Pass | Fail | Inconclusive | Then |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

### Detail on request
End with one line naming what the owner can ask for. Produce a block below only when asked.

#### Customer and offer fit
| Customer job, pain or gain | Priority and why | Current alternative | Offer part that serves it | Evidence tag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Drop or defer any offer part that serves no row.

#### Case for and against
Two short lists: strongest support, strongest countercase.

#### What changes the answer
List: observation, resulting change. Include the cost of being wrong.

### Always
- Judge the business and its appeal to venture capital separately.
- Records: carry assumptions and decisions into later work without copying whole transcripts.

Stop when further work is unlikely to change the decision, or propose one bounded next test.

When a specific unresolved question needs market research, demand validation, pricing, business economics or skeptical review:
- State the question with the useful evidence. The agent selects the expertise and combines the results.
- Invoke no other skill. Require none, and prescribe no chain.

Structure adapted in our own words from Value Proposition Design (Osterwalder and colleagues), The Lean Startup (Ries) and Disciplined Entrepreneurship (Aulet).
