# Business Model

Determine whether the proposed business can deliver its offer, earn an adequate return and fund the path there. Finish at the requested viability question.

## Terms
- **Binding constraint:** the limit that currently decides whether the business is viable.
- **Unit of analysis:** what the model counts, such as an engagement, order, active account or usage unit.
- **Realized price:** the price actually obtained after discounts.
- **Business engine:** the path from winning a customer, through delivery and repeat business, to collected cash.

## Always
1. Claim no demand or investment readiness from spreadsheets alone.
2. Treat a forecast as conditional, not as a predicted outcome.
3. Label evidence, assumptions and calculations next to their inputs.
4. Early paid work does not establish repeatable acquisition, retention or product-market fit.
5. Keep business economics separate from project commitments, and label simulated cases.

## Establish the question
Before modelling:
- Owner's decision: establish it as sustainable income, operating change, growth investment or venture-scale potential.
- Start from: customer, offer, payer, delivery process, pricing hypothesis and actual constraints.
- Evidence: accept what exists. Require no earlier skill run.
- Unit and period: choose both.
- Key unknown: identify which one can change viability before building a large model.

## Build the smallest useful business engine
- Order: calculate the binding driver first. Expand the model only if another driver can change viability.
- Reuse: take existing inputs with their units and dates. Do not rebuild a full spreadsheet.
- Averages: keep realized price, volume and mix separate, so discounts or a changing customer mix stay visible.
- Trace: acquisition channel and sales effort, conversion, onboarding, delivery, support and rework, repeat purchase or retention, cash collection.
- Costs: separate variable delivery, acquisition, fixed costs and founder time.
- Counting: count each cost once.
- Founder labor: unpaid time still has an economic cost.

When modelling, read [economics checks](references/economics.md).

When the offer needs two or more groups to join, treat each group as a customer:
- Join or refuse: state why each group would join and why it would refuse.
- Cost to win: estimate it for each group.
- Order: state which group to win first, and why.
- Test: test the groups together.

## Test scenarios
- Use: apply scenarios and sensitivities where they expose the drivers that matter.
- Coherence: keep related assumptions consistent. Do not combine every favorable estimate into an apparent base case.
- Break-even: show the requirement and compare it with reachable demand and capacity.

## Test execution and cash together
Check operational capacity as well as revenue.
- Hours: compare billable with available hours, and account for ramp-up and utilization.
- Cash cycle: account for the sales cycle and working capital.
- Dependencies: account for the reliability of suppliers and channels.
- Growth: if it exceeds delivery capacity or cash, the plan is not executable.
- Defensibility, distribution and expansion: assess them only as far as they change the requested decision.
- Ambition: distinguish sustainable small-business economics from venture-scale return potential.

## Report and stop

Use this structure for a substantial result. A short answer needs only the answer and its limit.

### Hard limits
- Whole result: 600 words at most, tables included. This is a limit, not a target.
- Three parts only: decision card, core table, closing table or list.
- Decision card: 200 words at most. Each line: three fragments at most, 12 words each.
- Core table: six rows at most. A template with more standard fields keeps every field. Closing part: four rows at most.
- Assumptions: one line under the table that uses them. No separate calculation walkthrough.
- Say each fact once across all three parts.
- One idea per table cell. Do not pack several rules or branches into a cell.
- Only warnings and stop conditions may push the result past 600 words.
- Too much to fit: keep what changes the decision. Drop the rest.

### Writing style
- Fragments and keywords. No introduction, recap, headline sentence or method notes.
- Short plain words a business owner understands. No unexplained shorthand.
- Evidence tags: Known, Supported, Assumed, Unknown, Contradicted.
- Unknown cell: write "Unknown". Never present a guess as fact.

### Part 1. Decision card
Always first. It covers only the immediate step, up to the owner's next decision. Write it as a two-column table, Line and Content, never as labelled paragraphs.

| Line | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Viability** | The answer and the binding constraint |
| Why | Up to three reasons |
| Against | The strongest case against your answer, even when you recommend stopping |
| Would change if | The observation that would change the answer, and what it would change |
| Next | Ordered actions in this step |
| Stop | What ends or pauses this step |
| At risk | Time, money, obligations, and the limit on the whole plan |
| Cost if wrong | What is lost if this answer is wrong, and who bears it |
| You decide | Approvals needed now, each with a default. A default grants no permission to spend, contact or launch |
| Warnings | One line each, for this step. Later-stage warnings sit beside their stage below |
| Unknown | Only what could change the answer |

The owner can complete the step from this card. Explain any check it names.

### Part 2. Scenarios and cash
| Scenario | Drivers changed | Break-even or capacity | Lowest cash and when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

State shared assumptions once above the table. Link a working model for longer calculations.

With this table, show the decisive inputs with their units, period and evidence or assumption tag. Use a compact input table when there are more than three.

### Part 3. What reverses the verdict
List: next observation, resulting change.

### Detail on request
End with one line naming what the owner can ask for. Produce a block below only when asked.

### Always
- Calculations: show formulas or link a working model. Do not repeat them in prose.
- Scenarios: compare only drivers that change the decision.

Stop at the requested viability boundary.

When a concrete uncertainty needs pricing, demand-validation or skeptical-review expertise:
- Name it and keep its inputs and assumptions with it. The agent decides whether to bring that expertise in.
- Invoke no other skill.
