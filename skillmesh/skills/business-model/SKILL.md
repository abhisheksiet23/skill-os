# Business Model

Start with the customer, offer, payer, delivery process, pricing hypothesis and actual constraints. Accept existing evidence without requiring a venture investigation or pricing skill first. Identify which unknown can change viability before building a large model.

Trace the business engine: acquisition channel and sales effort, conversion, onboarding, delivery, support/rework, repeat purchase or retention, and cash collection. Separate variable delivery costs, acquisition costs, fixed costs and founder time. Do not count the same cost twice or treat unpaid founder labor as economically free.

Use [economics checks](references/economics.md) when modelling. Keep units and periods explicit. Distinguish evidence, assumptions and calculations; show downside/base/upside ranges and sensitivities to the drivers that matter. A forecast is conditional, not a predicted outcome.

Check operational capacity as well as revenue. Account for billable versus available hours, ramp-up, utilization, sales cycle, working capital and reliability of suppliers/channels. Growth that exceeds delivery capacity or cash is not an executable plan.

Assess defensibility, distribution and expansion only to the extent they change the requested decision. Distinguish sustainable small-business economics from venture-scale return potential. Early paid work does not establish repeatable acquisition, retention or product-market fit.

Lead with the viability implication, then show a transparent model or concise calculation with units, assumption ranges and binding constraints. Prefer a compact scenario table or sensitivity chart when it makes comparison easier, but explain what drives the result and avoid invented precision. Keep business economics distinct from a project budget, schedule or delivery commitment. Use project conventions and label simulated company cases throughout. Stop when the requested viability question is answered within stated limits.

Recommend `business-pricing`, `business-validate` or `business-challenge` only for a concrete uncertainty. Recommendations preserve inputs and assumptions, but do not invoke skills. The agent owns selection and continuation.
