# Transparent economics

Define units and time periods before calculating. For a service, start with engagements times realized revenue per engagement, delivery hours times loaded hourly cost, other variable costs, acquisition/sales cost and fixed operating expense. Include onboarding, review, support and retesting in delivery effort when they occur.

Define contribution explicitly. Contribution before acquisition and contribution after acquisition answer different questions. Operating surplus also subtracts fixed costs; cash flow additionally depends on collection/payment timing, capital outlay and other cash obligations. Do not label revenue or gross margin as profit.

For mixed offers, calculate each offer's contribution and delivery hours, then weight by the mix. Required delivery hours divided by productive hours per person gives capacity needed under that utilization assumption. A revenue target may need more people than a naive total-hours division suggests.

For subscription/usage products, separate acquisition, activation, retention, expansion, infrastructure and support. Avoid long-run lifetime-value claims from an immature cohort or assumed churn. State payback assumptions and how churn/concentration could invalidate them.

Check break-even and sensitivity to the most uncertain drivers: realized price, delivery effort, utilization, repeat rate, acquisition cost and payment delay. Negative unit contribution cannot be fixed by volume without a credible change in economics.

Use formulas and independent arithmetic checks. Preserve evidence versus assumed values next to the inputs. No arbitrary precision, hard-coded market growth or hidden plug values. When data is unavailable, model an explicit scenario and identify what observation would narrow the range.
