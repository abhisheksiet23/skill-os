# Website Operate

Keep a launched website doing its job. Compare what is live with its baseline, report what changed and what it means, and recommend changes. Report observed evidence, not assumed results.

## Terms
- **Findability:** how readily search engines and AI assistants locate the site's pages and describe them accurately.
- **Launch baseline:** the dated record, taken at launch or at the first review, used for later comparison: source and date range, indexed pages, impressions and clicks, query list with positions, each AI question with its assistant and date, and technical state.
- **Crawler policy:** the owner's decision on which crawlers may access the site and for what purpose, such as search results, AI answers or AI training.
- **Snapshot:** a dated record of a search result or AI-assistant answer. It shows what appeared, not rankings, reach or influence.
- **Live fact:** a state verified on the live site, not one read from the repository or a plan.
- **Review:** one dated pass over the areas the owner requested.

## Frame the review
Do this first.
- Identify: the live URL, current revision, launch baseline and accepted decisions. From the handover, where recorded, also the operating owner, review cadence and whether recurring review is authorized.
- Authority: recurring or scheduled work needs the owner's authorization. This skill creates no monitoring obligation. Run it on request or on the owner's schedule.
- Access: list the sources available, such as search console, analytics, server logs, uptime checks and the enquiry destination. Missing access leaves its checks unverified.
- No baseline: capture one now, dated. State that earlier change cannot be attributed.
- Scope: choose the areas the request needs: findability, analytics and enquiries, technical health.

## Review findability
Read [search review](references/search-review.md).
- Compare: indexing, impressions, clicks and key queries with the baseline, the season and known site changes.
- Snapshots: repeat the baseline's AI-answer snapshots with the same assistants and questions.
- Report: change with its limits. Do not claim causation from a short window.
- Promises: never promise rankings, citations or traffic.

## Review analytics and enquiries
- Events: map each to what it establishes: visits, call-to-action engagement, attempted submission, accepted submission, received enquiry or qualified opportunity.
- Receipt: check delivery with authorized recipients. A call-to-action click is not a lead. A thank-you view is not receipt.
- Quality: report enquiry volume, spam and poor-fit rate where the owner can supply them.

## Review technical health
- Availability: uptime, TLS and domain expiry.
- Integrity: broken links and redirects, form delivery, email authentication.
- Maintenance: dependency and security updates, backups.
- Consent: tracking and cookies still match the privacy notice.
- Safety: make non-mutating checks. Live enquiry tests and any change need authorization.

## Decide and report
- Findings: rank by consequence. Give each its evidence, buyer impact and a practical correction.
- Changes: content, page or configuration changes need authority, and the review itself does not edit the live site.
- Records: record the review date, sources, snapshot set, findings, accepted decisions and unresolved dependencies. Add this review's figures as a new dated entry and keep the launch baseline unchanged, so later reviews can compare with it. Record only verified live facts as live.
- Form: lead with status. Then what changed since the last review, the evidence and named gaps.
- Out of scope: ongoing content programs, campaigns, paid media and conversion experiments need their own scope.
- Gaps: if another capability would resolve a named gap, such as page changes, content creation or an independent candidate review, describe the gap and the evidence that would resolve it.
