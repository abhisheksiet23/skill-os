# Choose control intensity by consequence

Choose a control because it protects a consequential outcome or enables a decision. Start with existing authority, evidence and records, then identify the actual gap. Combining responsibilities is useful when their purposes remain covered. A short contained correction usually needs its relevant check and update, not a new charter, schedule, risk register or review pipeline.

Increase attention for consequential uncertainty, irreversibility, external obligations, conflicting evidence or repeated failures. Reduce coordination ceremony for understood reversible work within delegation. Management time and owner attention are scarce resources too. Avoid a universal complexity score or process-count quota.

## Combine work while preserving authority and challenge

Analysis, implementation and tests may repeat the same mistaken interpretation. Ground important expectations in legitimate requirements and observed contracts, not solely in code or a test generated from that code. Real-boundary checks can challenge a shared mock assumption. Target fresh review, a different evidence source or operator/product judgment where that can change a consequential decision; do not double every task automatically.

A fresh agent conversation can reduce authoring-context bias but shares tools, files, instructions and possibly model assumptions. Do not claim independent organizational accountability or owner acceptance from agent consensus. Keep the authoring history, desired answer and unrelated session assessments out of bounded fresh-review inputs. Record missing evidence and incomplete review honestly.

Actual owner/product judgments, external permissions and legal/operational authority cannot be synthesized by assigning those roles to agents. Real stakeholders need attention only to the extent their interests/decisions affect delivery; absent evidence stays an assumption. Do not simulate interviews, negotiations, approvals or consent.

## Retain the responsibility; scale the ceremony

Retain outcome alignment, coherent planning, actual-performance assessment, grounded quality checks and legitimate closure. Combine communication, knowledge and integration work in existing records and targeted decisions. Adapt resource management to genuine capacity/access and multi-workstream conflicts. Omit human appraisal/motivation ceremonies where no human delivery team exists, while preserving coordination of actual contributors.

Activate supplier management for actual acquisition/contract obligations; compliance and adoption work for applicable consequences; formal assurance or quantitative risk analysis when justified. Do not omit important constraints because the project is small or automated. Do not add gates to meet a framework label.

At a milestone or observed failure, inspect what the control contributed: a changed decision, protected obligation, prevented rework or clearer owner understanding. A control can protect against a credible failure even when that failure did not occur; absence of an incident alone does not prove it was unnecessary. Simplify optional controls whose expected value no longer justifies upkeep. Respect adopted owner obligations and retain enough detail for useful visibility. Better-looking records alone do not demonstrate better delivery.

Background: [PMI tailoring guidance](https://www.pmi.org/-/media/pmi/documents/public/pdf/pmbok-standards/pmi-tailoring.pdf) supports adaptation and inspection; [PMI AI guidance](https://www.pmi.org/blog/ai-for-project-managers-beyond-quick-wins) preserves human accountability. This skill's operating choices are a tailored method, not a claim of official PMI compliance or certification.
