# Business Pricing

Establish who pays, what valuable outcome they buy, the current alternative/spend, usage variability and delivery cost. Use existing project evidence from any source. Ask only for consequential unavailable facts; label provisional assumptions rather than invent confidence.

Separate five decisions: payer, purchased value, price metric, package and price level. Compare only credible options for the buying situation, such as bounded engagements, usage, subscription, seats or outcomes. Evaluate predictability, measurement clarity, incentives, customer risk and supplier capacity. Define inclusions, exclusions, overages, retest/support and renewal terms where relevant.

Anchor hypotheses in observed alternatives, buyer value and cost-to-serve, not competitor price copying or cost-plus alone. Check effort variability, onboarding, acquisition, idle capacity and repeat work. A low-commitment offer can shift substantial risk to the seller.

For each serious option state target buyer, value, metric, package, reference point, proposed price/range, objection, economic assumptions and test. Use consistent units, currencies and tax treatment; do not imply legal or tax certainty without applicable evidence.

Test with recent spend, package choice and credible offers. Stated willingness to pay and survey techniques can inform hypotheses; actual purchase and repeat behavior provide stronger commercial evidence. Define success/failure before the test. Never fabricate survey results, demand or paid-pilot outcomes.

Lead with the option to test and why. Compare serious options compactly when that helps the buyer or owner see trade-offs, then state the evidence behind the recommendation, its commercial implication and what would change the choice. Distinguish recommendations from owner-approved commercial commitments. Store case facts in the project, not this skill. Finish at the requested pricing decision.

Missing demand evidence may justify recommending `business-validate`; uncertain economics may benefit from `business-model`. Explain the gap and relevant inputs. The agent decides whether to invoke another skill; this skill does not call skills or prescribe follow-on work.
