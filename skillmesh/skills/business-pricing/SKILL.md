# Business Pricing

Produce a price and package hypothesis the owner can credibly test, or assess an existing offer against the evidence. Finish at the requested pricing decision.

## Terms
- **Price metric:** the unit the buyer is charged for.
- **Package:** what the buyer gets for the price, with its limits and terms.
- **Cost-to-serve:** what it costs the seller to deliver to a buyer.
- **Contribution:** revenue minus the costs that the calculation names.
- **Realized price:** the price actually obtained after discounts.

## Always
1. Keep provisional options distinct from validated prices, and proposed pricing distinct from approved commitments.
2. Never fabricate survey results, demand or paid-pilot outcomes.
3. Designing a test does not authorize outreach, publication, charges or commercial commitments.
4. Imply no legal or tax certainty without applicable evidence.

## Establish the buying situation
Before shaping options:
- Establish: who pays, what valuable outcome they buy, the current alternative and spend, usage variability and delivery cost.
- Evidence: use what the project already has, including the current offer and known cost boundary.
- Questions: ask only for consequential facts that are unavailable. Label provisional assumptions.
- Investigation: pursue only unknowns that could change the package or its test.

## Design the options
Decide five things separately: payer, purchased value, price metric, package and price level.
- Options: compare only those credible for the buying situation, such as bounded engagements, usage, subscription, seats or outcomes.
- Compare on: predictability, measurement clarity, incentives, customer risk and supplier capacity.
- Terms of offer: where relevant, define inclusions, exclusions, overages, retest and support, and renewal.
- Anchor: use observed alternatives, buyer value and cost-to-serve. Neither copying competitor prices nor cost-plus is enough alone.
- Cost checks: effort variability, onboarding, acquisition, idle capacity and repeat work.
- Low commitment: check how much risk it shifts to the seller. It can be substantial.
- Outcome pricing: require a measurable outcome and a credible attribution boundary.

For each serious option, state every field of the section 6 options table.

- Bill: make it interpretable under ordinary and heavy usage.
- Risk: state who bears scope, consumption and outcome risk.

## Check the bill and the economics
Before recommending a package:
- Walk through: take one ordinary and one heavy buyer through the actual bill and seller contribution.
- Arithmetic: recompute decisive costs, bills and contribution from quantities and realized prices.
- Tool: use a calculator or executable expression when available.
- Reconcile: make the package table, invoice example, economics, table totals and narrative agree.
- Basis: state one basis for shared costs, units, currency and tax treatment. Put exceptions beside the affected option.

Worked example. These values are invented. Never reuse them as a recommended price.
- Offer: 200 currency units a month includes 100 jobs, then 3 per extra job.
- Costs: 0.8 variable cost per job and 30 account support cost.
- At 80 jobs: bill 200. Contribution before acquisition and fixed costs is 106.
- At 150 jobs: bill 350. Contribution on the same basis is 200.
- 20% off the entire heavy bill: revenue 280, contribution 130. Cost stays 150. It does not fall to 120.

## Check the commercial edges
Check any edge that could break the option:
- Units and limits: what counts as billable, resets or rollovers, usage limits and overage approval.
- Scope: included support and rework, and exclusions.
- Heavy usage matters: compare an explicit cap, paid overage and a larger package.
- Nominally unlimited plan: it needs a defensible cost boundary.
- Discounts: state basis, duration, renewal price and who may negotiate.
- Discount effect: test against contribution and capacity. Include concessions such as extra scope or payment delay.
- Introductory discount: keep it separate from the sustainable realized price.
- Unknown authority or terms: mark them as proposed owner decisions, not approved negotiation instructions.

## Test willingness to pay and cost to serve
When designing or assessing a test:
- Evidence: test with recent spend, package choice and credible offers.
- Strength: stated willingness to pay and survey techniques can inform hypotheses. Actual purchase and repeat behavior are stronger commercial evidence.
- Before the test: define success and failure.
- Include: relevant buyer eligibility, actual commitment and a delivery-cost guardrail.
- Track: offer exposure and rejection reasons.
- Low conversion: consider weak access, poor fit and unclear packaging as well as price.

## Report and stop

Write one business document, not a separate report for each question.

### One document
- Find first: use the project's business document or equivalent records. Otherwise create `business.md` in the project's records.
- Short question: answer it and its limit in chat. Create no document for it.
- No write access, or a read-only request: give the changed sections in chat.
- Read the whole document before changing it. Keep its IDs, history and wording that still holds.
- Add only sections that hold something useful. Number and order them as below.
- One home per item. Elsewhere, cite its section or ID.

| # | Section | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Decision and next steps | The one current summary |
| 2 | Current test | The one test: pass marks, limits, stops and results |
| 3 | Customers and offer fit | Needs linked to the offer |
| 4 | Alternatives and positioning | Choices and proof of difference |
| 5 | Reaching customers | Who, where, message, follow-up and effort |
| 6 | Offer and price | Packages, terms and bills |
| 7 | Business model | Canvas, numbers, capacity and cash |
| 8 | Assumptions and risks | One ordered list and the countercase |
| 9 | Records | Facts, owner decisions, open items, evidence, conflicts, replaced items |

### Your sections and others
- Yours: section 6.
- Write yours in full, using the templates below.
- Another section: add only supported information it lacks. Otherwise do not reword or shorten it.
- Test changes go in section 2. New risks go in section 8.
- Section 1: update it when your evidence changes the answer, actions or warnings. Keep one summary.

### Changing what is recorded
- Different judgment: add a conflict to section 9. Do not rewrite the other conclusion. Show it in section 1 while unresolved.
- Checked error: you recomputed it from recorded inputs, or confirmed it against its cited source.
- A different assumption or method is a judgment, not an error.
- Checked calculation or factual error: correct it where it appears, marked "Corrected" with the date. Update what depends on it.
- For each correction, record the old value, new value and basis in section 9.
- Error not yet checked: keep the value, flag it beside the value and record a conflict.
- Correction never covers owner statements, owner decisions, approved terms, pass marks or recorded results. Flag those and ask the owner.
- Pass marks: change them in section 2 only, before results. After results, start a new test and keep the old one.
- Replaced item: move it to the replaced list with the reason. Do not silently delete it.

### Records
- IDs: F fact, D owner decision, O open item, E evidence, X conflict, numbered, such as F-1. Keep an existing document's own scheme.
- Give each record its source and date.
- A recommendation never becomes an owner decision.

### Section 1 template
| Line | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Recommendation** | The current answer and its main reason |
| Why | Up to three reasons |
| Against | The strongest case against, even when recommending a stop |
| Would change if | The observation that would change the answer |
| At risk | Time, money, obligations and the limit on the whole plan |
| Cost if wrong | What is lost if the answer is wrong, and who bears it |
| Stop | What ends or pauses the current step |
| Unknown | Only what could change the answer |

Below the table:
- **Next, in order:** actions for the immediate step, up to the owner's next decision.
- **You decide:** approvals needed now, each with a default. A default grants no permission to spend, contact or launch.
- **Warnings:** one line each for this step.

The owner can complete the step from section 1 and the test it names.

### Evidence tags
| Tag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Known | Directly observed, or stated by the owner about their own business. Say which |
| Supported | Evidence points to it, within stated limits |
| Assumed | Not yet checked; used to proceed |
| Unknown | No basis yet |
| Contradicted | Evidence or arithmetic goes against it |

An owner's statement does not show how customers behave. Never present a guess as fact.

Calculated figures: mark them "calculated" and give them the evidence tag of their weakest input.

### Length and style
- Limits: section 1, 250 words. Sections 2 to 8, 400 words each. Tables count.
- May exceed a limit: warnings, stop conditions, the canvas and required records.
- Before saving: count each section you changed and cut it to its limit.
- Order: main choice first, then only what changes the decision.
- Calculations: show the deciding inputs and results in a table, each formula once.
- Detailed calculations and extra examples: add them only when the owner asks.
- Writing: fragments, lists and tables in plain words. A sentence only for cause, trade-off or warning.
- Leave out introductions, recaps and method notes.
- Table cell: one idea, 15 words at most.
- A later-stage warning sits beside its stage.

### Chat reply
After writing the document, reply in five short lines at most, one sentence each:
- Changed: what is new or different, and where.
- Answer: the current recommendation.
- Decide: the one decision the owner must make now.
- Watch: the most serious warning, only if one applies.
- Not verified: the biggest gap.

No tables, figures lists or reasons. The document holds them. Add the file path.

**Section 2:** add the price test, set up as in Test willingness to pay, with its next action.

**Only when it changes the decision or the owner asks:**
- Segments by need: outcome bought, alternative and its price, willingness-to-pay evidence and tag.
- Value against the next best alternative: reference price, what you add or lack, effect, tag.
- Features: leader drives the purchase, filler is nice to have, deal-killer reduces willingness to buy if bundled.

### Always
Stop at the requested decision.

When demand evidence is missing or the economics are uncertain:
- Name the gap and the inputs it needs. The agent decides whether to bring in demand-validation or business-economics expertise.
- Invoke no other skill. Prescribe no follow-on work.

Structure adapted in our own words from Monetizing Innovation (Ramanujam and Tacke) and The Strategy and Tactics of Pricing (Nagle and colleagues).

**Wording:** be truthful and growth-minded. Say "not yet" where something can still happen, and keep evidence, warnings and results exact.
