# Business Pricing

Produce a price and package hypothesis the owner can credibly test, or assess an existing offer against the evidence. Establish who pays, what valuable outcome they buy, the current alternative/spend, usage variability and delivery cost. Use existing project evidence; ask only for consequential unavailable facts and label provisional assumptions.

## Design the commercial choice

Separate five decisions: payer, purchased value, price metric, package and price level. Compare only credible options for the buying situation, such as bounded engagements, usage, subscription, seats or outcomes. Evaluate predictability, measurement clarity, incentives, customer risk and supplier capacity. Define inclusions, exclusions, overages, retest/support and renewal terms where relevant.

Anchor hypotheses in observed alternatives, buyer value and cost-to-serve, not competitor price copying or cost-plus alone. Check effort variability, onboarding, acquisition, idle capacity and repeat work. A low-commitment offer can shift substantial risk to the seller.

For each serious option state target buyer, value, metric, package, reference point, proposed price/range, objection, economic assumptions and test. Make the bill interpretable under ordinary and heavy usage; identify who bears scope, consumption and outcome risk. An outcome price needs a measurable outcome and a credible attribution boundary. Use consistent units, currencies and tax treatment; do not imply legal or tax certainty without applicable evidence.

## Test willingness to pay and cost to serve

Test with recent spend, package choice and credible offers. Stated willingness to pay and survey techniques can inform hypotheses; actual purchase and repeat behavior provide stronger commercial evidence. Define success/failure before the test, including relevant buyer eligibility, actual commitment and a delivery-cost guardrail. Track offer exposure and rejection reasons: low conversion can reflect weak access, poor fit or unclear packaging as well as price. Separate introductory discounts from sustainable realized price. Never fabricate survey results, demand or paid-pilot outcomes. Designing a test does not authorize outreach, publication, charges or commercial commitments.

## Make the next pricing decision explicit

Lead with the option to test and why, supporting evidence, commercial implications and what would change the choice. Compare serious options when it clarifies trade-offs. Distinguish recommendations from owner-approved commercial commitments. Keep case facts in project records and finish at the requested pricing decision.

Optionally recommend `business-validate` for missing demand evidence or `business-model` for uncertain economics. Explain the gap and inputs; the agent decides. Do not invoke skills or prescribe follow-on work.
