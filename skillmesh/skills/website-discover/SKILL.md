# Website Discover

Understand the business before proposing pages. Inspect supplied materials, current site, repository and existing decisions. Accept equivalent records from any source; no earlier skill is required.

Clarify what is sold, to whom, in what situation, with what scope and credible advantage. Separate user, buyer and decision-maker when relevant. Identify alternatives, objections, fit/exclusions, conversion and operational ownership. Use [interview guidance](references/interview.md) for consequential gaps and [research guidance](references/research.md) for externally answerable questions; do not load both by default.

Ask focused questions that change the website decision, with your interpretation and recommendation. Preserve prior answers and delegated authority. Owner knowledge is useful evidence but not proof of market-wide demand. Resolve ambiguous offers with the owner rather than inventing prices, guarantees or commitments.

Inventory authentic claims, work, credentials, testimonials, imagery and publication permission early. Never fill gaps with invented proof. Where evidence is sparse, an honest story about real scope, method, people and constraints can still be useful.

Recommend audience, buying situation, central promise, differentiation, objections, page/journey implications and valuable conversion. Tie consequential claims to sources and limitations. Keep private customer material out of public research tools and deployable content.

Save a concise brief in existing project records: evidence, decisions, assumptions, usable assets and unresolved questions. Finish when the requested positioning decision is useful and its limits are clear; research need not become exhaustive.

If demand uncertainty blocks the offer, recommend the relevant validation question, optionally naming `business-validate`. If expression is the remaining need, `website-create` may help. These are optional recommendations to the agent; this skill neither invokes others nor mandates continuation.
