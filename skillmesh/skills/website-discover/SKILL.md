# Website Discover

Produce a usable website position: who the site serves, the buying situation, the credible promise, the evidence and the next action. The brief explains page and journey choices. It does not merely describe the company.

## Terms
- **Findability:** how readily search engines and AI assistants locate the site's pages and describe them accurately.
- **Position:** the recommended audience, buying situation, promise, differentiation, objections and conversion, with their consequences for the site.
- **Hypothesis:** a stated assumption, testable by evidence, carried forward until evidence replaces it.
- **Accepted decision:** an owner choice already made. Recommendations, assumptions and open dependencies are kept apart from it.
- **Target queries:** the buyer searches and questions each important page should answer, listed per page with the evidence for each.

## Recover what is already known
Do this first, for every engagement.
- Sources: inspect supplied material, the current site, the repository and accepted decisions. Use adequate records from any author.
- Clarify: offer and delivery scope, audience, trigger, alternatives, objections, fit and exclusions, and who owns conversion.
- Search behavior: how buyers currently search for and ask about this offer, including search engines, AI assistants, referrals and directories.
- Roles: distinguish users, buyers and decision-makers only where their needs change the story.
- Inventory early: claim support and usable assets: work, credentials, testimonials, imagery, source context and publication permission.
- Young companies: tell an honest story through scope, method, people and constraints. Do not invent proof.

## Resolve the uncertainty that changes the site
Choose the evidence method from the missing fact.
- Owner-dependent knowledge: hold a focused conversation.
- Public category or competitor questions: research them.
- Low-risk details: keep them as explicit hypotheses.

Reuse before asking:
- Carry forward accepted answers and adequately sourced findings, including business findings on buyers, alternatives, price and demand, while their date, market and scope still apply. A hypothesis stays a hypothesis.
- Investigate the gap that could change the site. Do not recreate the whole brief.
- Do not ask the owner to repeat supplied answers.
- Do not infer unapproved prices, guarantees or commitments.

References, loaded only for the unresolved question:
- Consequential owner gaps: inspect the headings of the [interview bank](references/interview.md). Read its method, the buying-episode method when buyer motivations are uncertain, exit discipline and the relevant question sections.
- Questions to the owner: lead with the current interpretation and, when supported, a recommendation. Preserve delegated authority.
- Externally answerable questions: read [research guidance](references/research.md). Choose sources that test a decision, including contradictory evidence. Do not research merely to fill a section.

Privacy: keep private customer material out of public research tools and deployable content.

## Deliver a position someone can use
- Lead: the recommended audience, buying situation, promise, differentiation, objections and conversion.
- Then show: their consequences for page priority, narrative, proof and creative direction.
- Findability: include target queries when the site must be found through search or AI assistants.
- Form: use descriptive labels and short lists. Use a compact comparison table for competing positions when it helps the decision.
- Reasoning: keep the reasoning and qualifications needed to support real copy. Do not deliver an unexplained keyword list.
- Sources: put sources and limits beside consequential conclusions.
- Records: in existing project records, keep accepted decisions, recommendations, assumptions, usable assets and open dependencies separate.
- Stop: when the requested positioning decision is useful and its remaining limits are clear.
- Gaps: recommend the evidence gap. Category or competitor questions may call for market research. A stated price may call for pricing work, and unresolved demand for demand validation. Expression may call for copy and design work.

**Wording:** be truthful and growth-minded. Say "not yet" where something can still happen, and keep evidence, warnings and results exact.
