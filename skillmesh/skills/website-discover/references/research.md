# Research that changes decisions

Research only when it can change a website decision. Choose sources that could weaken a hypothesis as well as support it.

## Terms
- **Evidence chain:** source and access date, observed fact, its limits, inference and confidence, then the website implication and any follow-up.
- **Snapshot:** a dated record of a search result, AI-assistant answer, review or competitor claim. It shows what appeared, not performance, demand, rankings or influence.

## Frame the investigation
- Decision first: name the website decision before searching.
- Hypotheses: translate it into a few consequential hypotheses about a buyer trigger, objection, alternative, trust requirement, search intent or category convention.
- Existing evidence: adequate evidence needs no new search. If creative work reveals a gap, investigate that gap and do not reopen the whole brief.
- Sequencing: resolve uncertainty before building work that depends on it. Independent, honest content can proceed while an unrelated question stays open.

Source reliability:
- Official company sources: what an organization says it offers.
- Direct buyer evidence: specific experiences.
- Credible independent sources: broader context.
- Competitor marketing claims: evidence of its messaging, not of its performance.
- Reviews: selective observations, not a representative survey.

## Investigate
- Company: current site, supplied materials, service boundaries, inconsistencies, actual differentiators, missing proof.
- Market: a purposeful sample of direct competitors and alternative solutions, including doing nothing or handling the work internally.
- Buyers: public questions and reviews, plus authorized private evidence. Capture language, evaluation criteria, objections and information gaps. Protect private identities.
- Search: intents and observed result types for relevant services and buying situations. Record query, region and language when known, date and source. Never invent search volume, rankings or traffic. Observed results are snapshots.
- AI answers: for a few representative buyer questions, record the assistant, the exact question, the date, what the answer says about the category and whether it names the company or competitors.
- AI answers unavailable: If no AI assistant can be queried, report the result as unknown. Never record an answer you did not observe. Your own reply is not an observation.
- AI answer limits: answers vary between runs. They show how the category is described, not how many people see it.
- Authority sources: the third-party sources buyers and assistants rely on, such as directories, reviews, publications and associations. Note where the company is missing or described inaccurately.
- Category expectations: the information visitors need to assess fit and trust. Explain when following a convention helps and when it creates sameness.
- Creative references: inspect actual pages and images when judging design, including relevant adjacent industries. Search snippets cannot prove visual quality. Name the principle to learn, not a layout to clone.
- Redesign: known landing pages, content inventory, navigation, conversion friction, integration behavior and URLs to preserve. Use available analytics and search data with authorization. Lack of access is not evidence of zero value.

How much:
- Scale source count to uncertainty and source quality.
- Stop when findings stop changing decisions.
- Do not stop while a material contradiction remains unexplored just because a quota has been met.

## Convert evidence to action
- Chain: turn findings into decisions through the evidence chain. Keep exact observations distinct from interpretation. Quote sparingly and accurately.
- Usefulness: a long source list with no effect on copy, priority, proof or structure is not a synthesis.
- Disagreement: when sources disagree, identify whether the difference concerns audience, date, geography, offer or evidence quality before combining them.
- Contradictions: preserve an unresolved material contradiction. Recommend a proportionate next decision or evidence request.
- Change: explain what changed from the initial hypothesis.
- Purpose: a research brief should enable a strategy conversation, not overwhelm the owner with links.

An adequate synthesis answers:
1. What do visitors need to recognize quickly?
2. What is this company credibly entitled to say differently?
3. Which original assumption weakened or changed?
4. What evidence or owner decision is still missing?
5. How does this change priority, copy, page structure, proof or creative direction?

## Limitations
- Public research cannot establish private economics, actual service capacity, publication permissions or undisclosed customer motivations. Ask the owner.
- Rankings, citations and traffic of a new or changed site cannot be established before launch. Report them as unknown. Never promise rankings, citations or traffic.
- Browsing unavailable: work from supplied sources and label external research pending. Do not invent citations or imply that competitor analysis occurred.
