# Research that changes decisions

## Frame the investigation

Start with a few consequential hypotheses: buyer trigger, main objection, alternative considered, trust requirement, search intent, or misunderstood category convention. Choose sources to test them. Research before production building; repeat narrowly when creative exploration reveals a gap.

Use official company sources for what an organization says it offers; direct buyer evidence for specific experiences; credible independent sources for broader context. A competitor's marketing claim is evidence of its messaging, not proof of its performance. Reviews are selective observations, not a representative survey.

## Investigate

- Company: current site, supplied materials, service boundaries, inconsistencies, actual differentiators, missing proof.
- Market: a purposeful sample of direct competitors and alternative solutions, including doing nothing or handling the work internally.
- Buyers: public questions/reviews and authorized private evidence; language, evaluation criteria, objections, and information gaps. Protect private identities.
- Search: intents and observed result types for relevant services and buying situations. Record query, region/language when known, date, and source. Never invent search volume, rankings, or traffic; observed results are contextual snapshots.
- Category expectations: information visitors need to assess fit and trust. Explain when following a convention helps and when it creates sameness.
- Creative references: inspect actual pages/images when judging design, including relevant adjacent industries. Search snippets cannot prove visual quality. Name the principle to learn, not a layout to clone.
- Redesign: known landing pages, content inventory, navigation, conversion friction, integration behavior, and URLs to preserve. Use available analytics/search data with authorization; lack of access is not evidence of zero value.

Scale source count to uncertainty and source quality. Avoid exhaustive research when findings stop changing decisions. Do not stop while a material contradiction remains unexplored merely because a quota has been met.

## Convert evidence to action

For each consequential finding, record source/link, access date, observation, limits, inference, confidence, website implication, and follow-up if needed. Quote sparingly and accurately. Separate what the source says from what you infer.

An adequate synthesis answers:

1. What do visitors need to recognize quickly?
2. What is this company credibly entitled to say differently?
3. Which original assumption weakened or changed?
4. What evidence or owner decision is still missing?
5. How does this change priority, copy, page structure, proof, or creative direction?

If evidence conflicts, describe the conflict and recommend a decision proportionate to its importance. A research brief should support a strategy discussion, not overwhelm the owner with links.

## Limitations

Public research cannot establish private economics, actual service capacity, publication permissions, or undisclosed customer motivations. Ask the owner. If browsing is unavailable, work from supplied sources and label external research pending; do not invent citations or imply that competitor analysis occurred.
