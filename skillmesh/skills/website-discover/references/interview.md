# Adaptive interview

Use this as a question bank, not a form to send wholesale. Start with a synthesis grounded in supplied material. Select the questions whose answers would change positioning, scope, page structure, trust, conversion, or art direction. Ask related questions together and avoid burdening the owner with facts public research can establish.

## Conduct each round

1. State the current interpretation and its evidence.
2. Identify the consequential uncertainty or contradiction.
3. Offer a recommendation when enough evidence exists.
4. Ask concrete questions about actual situations, examples, and decisions.
5. Record the answer once; update downstream implications.

Useful pattern: "Your deck leads with speed, but your customer examples emphasize avoiding disruption. I recommend leading with reliability. In the last few wins, what did buyers actually say tipped the decision?"

When the owner cannot answer, suggest what evidence could resolve it or proceed with a clearly bounded hypothesis. "Everyone" as an audience calls for prioritizing buying situations, not silently inventing a persona. A request for minimal questions changes interview length, not permission to fabricate.

## Assignment and success

- Why is this website needed now? What changed?
- What should become easier for the business and for visitors?
- Which outcome matters most: qualified enquiries, purchases elsewhere, credibility during sales, recruitment, partner interest, or another purpose?
- What would make this project unsuccessful despite an attractive website?
- Is there an existing deadline, launch dependency, or fixed budget that changes scope?
- Which decisions should the AI own, and which do you want to review?

## Offer and delivery

- What exactly does a customer receive, and what is outside the engagement?
- Which offers matter most strategically or commercially?
- Are services bought separately, bundled, or sequenced?
- What needs to be true for your service to be a good fit?
- What happens from first conversation through delivery and ongoing support?
- What can you confidently promise, and what depends on assessment?
- Is pricing public, indicative, fixed, or quotation-based? What may be published?
- Are there capacity, geographical, regulatory, or delivery constraints visitors need to understand?

## Buyers and buying situations

- Describe a recent ideal customer and the situation that brought them to you.
- Who initiates, evaluates, approves, pays for, and uses the service?
- What event makes the problem urgent enough to act on?
- How do prospects describe the problem before learning your terminology?
- What have they tried already? What would they do if they did not hire you?
- Which customers should the website discourage or redirect?
- Where do useful visitors come from: referrals, search, campaigns, outbound sales, or existing relationships?
- Do different audiences need separate paths, or can one prioritized story serve them?

## Decisions, objections, and evidence

- What questions recur in sales conversations?
- What makes a prospect hesitate, delay, or choose another option?
- Why did you win the last few suitable engagements? What evidence supports that interpretation?
- What can a customer verify before speaking to you?
- Which results can be published, with what context and permission?
- Are testimonials approved and attributable? May customer names and logos be used?
- Do certifications apply to the company, a person, a product, or a particular period?
- Which claims in existing materials are outdated, disputed, or difficult to substantiate?

## Conversion and operations

- What is the most useful next step for a visitor who is ready?
- What should someone do if interested but not ready for a conversation?
- What information is genuinely needed to qualify or route an enquiry?
- Who receives it, where, and what happens next?
- Is any response-time commitment already operationally supported?
- Are bookings immediate or subject to qualification/availability?
- What alternative should visitors have if a form or booking service fails?
- How will you distinguish a valuable enquiry from spam or a poor-fit lead?

## Brand and creative direction

- What should visitors feel about the company, and why would that be believable?
- Which existing brand elements must remain?
- Which words or visual treatments would misrepresent the company?
- Which references appeal to you, and which specific qualities matter?
- Should the company feel established, approachable, technical, editorial, expressive, or something else supported by its position?
- What real people, work, processes, or environments can we show?
- Do assets exist in usable quality, with publication rights?
- If photography or case studies are unavailable, can we obtain them or choose another honest direction?

## Existing site and maintenance

- Which pages, resources, URLs, forms, or integrations are important today?
- Are there known high-value search landing pages or campaign destinations?
- What is currently confusing or failing, and what evidence shows that?
- Who will edit content, what will change frequently, and what tools can they maintain?
- Are multilingual content, local markets, or accessibility needs in scope?
- Who owns the domain, hosting, analytics, and destination systems?
- Are there contractual, privacy, or industry content requirements needing supplied wording or specialist review?
- What existing measurements can establish a baseline without collecting unnecessary personal information?

## Exit discipline

Classify remaining gaps as: consequential owner decision; researchable question; implementation dependency; or low-risk assumption. Resolve the first two enough to avoid speculative positioning. Do not block honest work merely because a young company lacks case studies. Save only answers and reasoning that affect the website.
