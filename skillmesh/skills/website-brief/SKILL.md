# Website Brief

Turn a broad website request into one coherent visitor experience: a credible offer, a buyer story, distinctive expression and a working route to a business outcome. Supply whole-site judgment without imposing a sequence of specialist engagements.

## Terms
- **Findability:** how readily search engines and AI assistants locate the site's pages and describe them accurately.
- **Join:** a point where two parts of the site must agree, such as promise and service, proof and claim, or call to action and operation.
- **Reserved decision:** a choice the owner has not delegated. Silence does not make a decision reserved.

## Establish the site decision
Start here for any broad website request.
- Read: company material and adequate existing decisions. Then inspect the current site and repository.
- Determine: who arrives, what makes the offer relevant now, what evidence helps them decide and what useful next action the business can support. Include how buyers search and ask, including AI assistants.
- Include: alternatives, fit and exclusions, operating ownership and constraints.
- Scope: separate a website assignment from changing the business itself.
- Preserve: valuable content, URLs, integrations and editing workflows.

## Choose the next work
Pick it from the uncertainty most likely to invalidate the result.
- Unclear offer: clarify it first, because every later page depends on it.
- Approved design: implement it. Do not redesign it without a reason.
- Broken form: repair and retest it, because a failed conversion path loses enquiries.
- Reuse: accepted direction, usable assets and candidate-bound proof, while their assumptions still hold. Reopen only what the change invalidates.
- Contained correction to an established site: do not restart positioning or build a fresh prototype.
- Work spanning several concerns: keep their decisions connected. Do not hand back separate reports.

## Make the direction concrete
Use this to test the direction on real journeys before extending it.
- Check: inspect a representative responsive journey before extending the pattern across the site. A framework choice, attractive hero or successful build does not establish the experience.
- Detail: page-level copy, art direction and prototyping are design work. Recommend it as a gap when needed.
- Entry pages: trace the buyer argument across likely entry pages, not just navigation order.
- Entry differences: a referral visitor may need scope and availability. A search visitor may need relevance and credibility. Let that change entry-page depth and links.
- Repetition: do not repeat the whole homepage on every page.

Where the joins are uncertain, use this working map. It is a diagnostic, not a required page template.

| Argument | Decision to make | Cross-page/operating check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Findability | Which searches and questions should reach which page? | Page headline, title and opening answer match the intent. The page is crawlable and indexed as intended. |
| Promise | What useful change can this buyer expect? | Search or campaign wording, page headline and actual service describe the same offer. |
| Proof | Why believe this promise here? | Evidence is close to the claim, attributable and qualified. A linked case supports the relevant outcome. |
| Fit | Who should proceed or choose another route? | Service scope, exclusions and practical constraints survive into detail pages and qualification. |
| Objection | What could reasonably stop this purchase? | Relevant process, risk, comparison or pricing information appears before the decision that needs it. |
| Action | What happens when the visitor acts? | Call to action, form, confirmation, receiving operator and follow-up agree on the commitment. |

## Judge the joins
Use this when promise, proof, fit or action may disagree.
- Questions: does the promise match the service? Does proof support that promise? Does the call to action set correct expectations? Can the business receive and handle the resulting action?
- Together: evaluate buyer clarity, visual craft, accessible interaction and operational readiness as one.
- Failing join: fix the underlying promise, evidence or operation before polishing its wording. "Book a consultation" needs an actual booking or a clear request-and-confirm process. A generic thank-you cannot settle which one the business offers.
- Conversion path: distinguish UI feedback, provider acceptance and actual receipt.

## Claims
Apply this to every claim, price, guarantee and image on the site.
- Use only supportable claims, prices, guarantees, locations and delivery commitments.
- Never promise rankings, citations or traffic.
- Separate owner statements, external evidence, assumptions and permission to publish.
- Conceptual imagery and simulated forms must be recognizable as such. Neither supplies documentary or delivery proof.

## Complete at the requested boundary
Use this when closing out any request.
- Primary: keep the site or concrete direction primary.
- Explain: consequential page and journey choices through buyer evidence and the action they support.
- Form: use meaningful labels and short lists for decisions, proof and dependencies. Use a comparison table when it makes an actual choice easier. Keep the buyer argument and its qualifications intact. Do not reduce website copy to keywords.
- Records: in existing project records, distinguish accepted decisions, the reviewed candidate, live facts and unresolved dependencies.
- Evidence: report what was actually inspected and tested. Missing evidence remains a gap.
- Authority: honor delegated authority and existing approvals. Pause only dependent work for a reserved decision.
- Publication: preparation alone does not authorize publication or messages to people. Purchases are never made.
- Gaps: for a gap that needs specialist depth, recommend the question and the evidence that would resolve it. The categories are positioning, narrative and design, implementation, candidate assessment, authorized launch, post-launch operation and demand evidence.
